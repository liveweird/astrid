# Seven-Module Danish Vocabulary Course Blueprint

## Status and relationship to grammar

**8 November 2026 — User-supplied course direction and proposed inventory.** Organize vocabulary through situational and functional themes synchronized with the [seven grammar modules](grammar-course-blueprint.md). The user supplied 35 thematic units, moving from immediate identity and daily transactions through personal experience and society to abstract/professional discourse. The scope below preserves that inventory and the later lesson-composition proposal; examples are candidate teaching content requiring review, not newly activated lexicon.

The A1.1–B2 bands are the user's proposed navigation labels, not validated CEFR alignment or a guarantee of proficiency. The 28 grammar lessons are confirmed to be topic units with multiple short sessions (Q26). Applying the same multi-session interpretation to vocabulary units is an assistant working assumption; no per-session item count, total vocabulary quota or completion threshold has been agreed.

## User's core principles

1. **Situational scripts over taxonomic lists:** mix word classes in useful situations. The supermarket example combines `en kurv, et æble, en kvittering`, actions `at betale, at veje, at lede efter`, and modifiers `frisk, udsolgt, billig`.
2. **Grammar-synchronized selection:** Module 2 should emphasize concrete countable objects supporting gender, definiteness and plurals; Module 5 should support direction/location and posture through interiors, furniture and navigation. Themes should supply material the learner can immediately use with the current grammar.
3. **Mandatory morphological bundling:** the user requests that nouns and verbs not be taught as bare isolated forms, and specifically that nouns be learned with their indefinite article from first exposure (`en bil`, `et hus`). The precise verb teaching bundle, treatment of non-count nouns, and scope of noun-only assessment need explicit authoring rules. The distinction between teaching presentation and current E008 noun-only recall is open in Q27.
4. **Compounds and collocations:** teach reusable word-building patterns and constructions, including `sygehus`, `arbejdsløshedsforsikring`, `at glæde sig til`, and `at holde af`. The user's “Lego” metaphor motivates decoding, not an observed guarantee that knowing roots automatically supplies thousands of usable words.

The user motivates thematic presentation through lexical interference when many narrowly related forms are introduced together. Evidence and the tension with some proposed unit headings are recorded below; a universal “most effective” claim is not established.

## Anatomy of a vocabulary lesson — user proposal

| Teaching-item category | Approximate share | Proposed items |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core entities: nouns | 40% | 6–8 |
| Actions and processes: verbs | 25% | 4–5 |
| Qualities and modifiers: adjectives/adverbs | 20% | 3–4 |
| Functional chunks: phrases | 15% | 2–3 |

The ranges total **15–20 teaching items**; the 20-item example is exactly 8 nouns, 5 verbs, 4 modifiers and 3 chunks. The percentages are approximate authoring guidance, not researched optimal proportions or an agreed hard gate. A chunk can contain a noun or verb: count it once as an assigned teaching item instead of double-counting its component words. Reused entries should be distinguished from newly introduced entries.

**Assistant recommendation:** apply the mix across a topic unit and introduce small batches through several short sessions. Vary it where the task needs more function words, verbs or chunks. Numbers/calendar coverage, the proposed 25-verb pool and dense adjective/body-part sets need subdivision; the headings are not instructions to introduce all listed material simultaneously. Across 35 units the ranges imply 525–700 item placements if applied once per unit, not an established unique-word inventory or B2 vocabulary target. Q28 keeps the scope of the item budget explicit.

## Evidence and authoring recommendations

**Lexical interference — bounded evidence:** Tinkham's 1997 experiments support distinguishing narrow semantic sets from thematic associations; the publisher abstract reports hindrance for the former and facilitation for the latter. A later controlled study found more interference errors for related items but no significant related/unrelated difference in posttest scores; spacing benefited unrelated items more. These tasks do not prove that a particular Danish theme sequence is universally best or establish Astrid's learner outcomes. [Tinkham, 1997, publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1191/026765897672376469); [Nakata & Suzuki, 2019, author-hosted full paper](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Nakata-Suzuki-2019-SSLA.pdf), DOI `10.1017/S0272263118000219`.

The following are assistant proposals to reconcile the inventory with the user's principles:

- Keep units such as colors/opposites/body parts as navigation and later contrast/review themes. First introduce a small mixed set through a task, revisit it in other situations, then contrast related words deliberately. Semantic tags remain useful for targeted distractors; initial presentation and later assessment serve different purposes.
- For countable noun senses, teach the article+noun bundle from first exposure. Keep lemma, gender, article, countability and forms separately in the data so the bundle can be rendered appropriately. Mass/plural-only uses need their own presentation: food units already include `mælk/kaffe`, and family includes plural `søskende`. Article bundling must not turn into “insert an indefinite article before every noun in every sentence.” DSN documents gender and mass-use variation; author the selected sense and context explicitly. [RO §20](https://ro.dsn.dk/?paragraf=20&type=rulesearch); existing [seed boundaries](danish-vocabulary-seed.md#nouns-and-selected-meanings).
- Define a verb packet as a selected meaning, infinitive, introduced finite forms, required complements/prepositions and a short licensed example. Show irregular forms deliberately; keep later tense forms in reference metadata until taught. `at` is part of an infinitive teaching label, not a prefix for every finite/modal sentence. Multiword/reflexive constructions need slots that can inflect with the subject, not immutable strings.
- Revisit situations across grammar modules: basic home/transport/health expressions may be useful early, with spatial detail, narratives and institutional language added later. Teach useful functional chunks before demanding full analysis, while distinguishing supplied support from assessed grammar. A public-service or culture theme must not imply that every learner shares the same lifestyle or needs.
- Decode reviewed compounds and derivations, but teach lexical restrictions, linking elements and nonliteral meanings. Do not automatically generate every root/affix combination or count inferred meanings as learned vocabulary. Teach transparent short examples early; reserve the detailed morphology unit for later analysis.
- Give each unit an observable reading/writing outcome: understand a short message, extract a needed fact, or write a relevant response using its grammar. Assess recognition and recall separately, retain raw answers and uncertainty, and test transfer beyond the original theme. No adaptive scheduler or mastery threshold is selected.
- Treat health, civic, employment and financial examples as language-learning scenarios. Before publishing real-world procedural claims or branded-service examples, check current authoritative sources and record the date. Cultural descriptions and slang need context and register labels; conversational content can be rendered in writing without activating listening/speaking work.
