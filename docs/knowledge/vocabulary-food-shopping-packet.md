# P2 Vocabulary Authoring Packet: Food, Drink and a Small Purchase

## Status and boundary

This is the bounded P2 continuation of the [selected vocabulary expansion
plan](vocabulary-course-blueprint.md#selected-expansion-authoring-plan--11-november-2026). It proposes
**exactly 21 selected-meaning or chunk placements** and six short session outlines with introduction
batches of **`4 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 3`**. The intended transfer outcome is to read a short shop/café
exchange, distinguish products and supplied quantities, and assemble one constrained request.

This packet is authoring material, not runtime activation. The running private preview remains app
0.19.0 on learning pair sixteen with 40 lexical records. No stable lexical or sense IDs are allocated
here, no current generator or grader is broadened, and no Danish specialist approval is claimed.
Existing E007/E008 recall is fixed to its noun-only contract. Guided matching is not currently wired
for this packet; matching, dialogue selection and quantity/request tiles below are planned authoring
formats, not claims about present capabilities. Q27 and article-bundle assessment remain open and are
not decided here.

The blueprint's `6 reuse + 15 new` split is retained exactly. Reuse means the same selected meaning,
not a duplicate identity: current `L012-S01`, `L021-S01`, `L034-S01` and P1 rows `P1-13`–`P1-15`
remain their owners. The fifteen candidates include a new request sense/frame for the existing `have`
lemma; it must not reuse possession sense `L009-S01`. This mix was chosen for a small real situation,
not as a food taxonomy or frequency list.

### Count, mass and placement audit

- Reuse: **6** (`P2-01`, `P2-02`, `P2-05`–`P2-07`, `P2-18`). New candidates:
  **15** (all other rows). Total: **21**.
- Count bundles: `et æble`, `en bolle`, `en butik`, `en café`, `en kop`, `en flaske`.
  The article is taught with the noun but is not independently assessed.
- Bare mass uses: `brød, kaffe, vand, mælk`. COR noun gender does not turn
  these selected substance uses into early `en/et + noun` targets. No synthetic article is added.
- Verbal placements are `spise, drikke, købe, betale`, reused modal `ville → vil`, and the distinct
  request-frame use of bare `have`. `billig/gerne` and `undskyld/værsgo/tak` supply modifiers
  and functional language. The nominal/action/modifier/function mix is 10/6/2/3 placements,
  approximately 48%/29%/10%/14%; the user’s mix is guidance rather than a per-session quota.
  This replaces three proposed product/drink synonyms with situational functions, limiting new
  same-category interference while preserving 21 placements and the 6/15 reuse/new split.

### Grammar/support dependency ledger

1. **Already available but still scoped:** human subject pronouns; finite present forms; the stored
   ability/intention bare-infinitive pattern; and articles supplied with known count nouns. Reuse does
   not broaden their old construction permissions.
2. **Needed before productive P2 assessment:** explicit teaching that the four selected substance
   uses are bare while the six selected count bundles include `en/et`; exposure tracking must be tied
   to the packet/session revision. This does not settle Q27 or create article-bundle assessment.
3. **Later grammar, held out:** plural and definite nouns, numbers and productive quantities, prices
   and currency, adjective agreement (`varm/kold/stor/lille`), productive location syntax, and request
   alternatives such as `Må jeg få …?` or finite `Jeg har …`. Their absence must not be graded as bad
   Danish outside the packet.
4. **Supplied support, not hidden placements:** `i en butik/i en café`, punctuation,
   speaker labels and English situation prompts. If any becomes assessed, it needs its own identity,
   introduction and source review.
5. **Bounded new labels/functions:** P2-04 `billig` is a labelled recognition item, not a
   productive adjective phrase or agreement target. P2-09/P2-10 are complete interjections
   with English-authored attention/handover context; no imperative grammar follows.

## Required direct-entry support

Every session must visibly teach its own selected forms and review all reused prerequisites before
assessment, with English meanings and full translations of its reading. The first person `jeg = I`,
names, `Det er … = this is …`, and any supplied location/quantity statement are explicit support.
No completion of P1 or another P2 session is inferred.

| Session | Required review beyond its new batch |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | `jeg = I`, Nora is a name; supplied subject–finite-verb–object order; count versus bare food use. |
| 2 | `jeg = I`, Anna/Nora are names; the selected drink frame and all three English beverage meanings. |
| 3 | `spiser = eats`, `brød = bread`, names and identification; translate both functional scenes explicitly. |
| 4 | `en bolle = a bread roll`, `jeg = I`, `spiser/drikker = eats/drinks`; show and translate the complete purchase/payment sequence and intact café location before tiles. |
| 5 | `købe = buy`, `en bolle = a bread roll`, `jeg = I`; bare infinitive after supplied `vil`; review `undskyld/værsgo` with their scenes before the gratitude choice. |
| 6 | `vil/gerne`, `kaffe/vand`, `tak`, `jeg = I`, Nora is a name; show and translate both exact quantity requests and contrast request `have` with possession support `har = has`. |

For session 4, the English situation explicitly orders the purchase then payment; sentence order
alone is not treated as a universal chronology rule. Session 6 contains two parallel request notes,
not a question–reply dialogue. Full future scripts must retain those distinctions.

## Six short session outlines

Each outline names a future authoring slot and answer. These are not executable current plans. Later
sessions may review earlier rows without creating additional placements. All Danish readings and
exercise wording below are original draft material awaiting teaching review.

### Session 1 — A small breakfast (`4`: P2-01–P2-04)

**Real-life outcome:** understand what two people eat and recognize a supplied inexpensive-product
label. Teach `at spise → spiser`, `et æble`, bare `brød`, and base-form label `billig`. The label's
English scene explicitly concerns a low-priced product; there is no adjective agreement exercise.

Reading:

> Jeg spiser et æble.  
> Nora spiser brød.

Planned slots:

1. Three-pair matching: `et æble ↔ an apple`, `brød ↔ bread`, `spiser ↔ eats/is eating`.
2. Label recognition: `billig` → **inexpensive**, with English property alternatives
   `inexpensive / expensive / fresh`. English alternatives do not introduce their Danish forms.
3. Reading choice: “What does Nora eat?” → **bread**, with **an apple** as the other stated food.

Exposure rule: both full readings and English translations are required teaching. `billig` remains
an isolated supplied quality label; `billigt/billige`, adjective placement and agreement are held out.

### Session 2 — Drinks on the table (`4`: P2-05–P2-08)

**Real-life outcome:** understand the drink verb and three everyday beverage words in a simple “who
drinks what” note.

Teaching presents `at drikke → drikker` and bare `kaffe, vand, mælk`; it explicitly reuses the first
two P1 beverages without allocating new identities.

Reading:

> Anna drikker kaffe.  
> Jeg drikker vand. Nora drikker mælk.

Planned slots:

1. Danish→English recognition: `mælk` → **milk**; answer `milk` among `coffee / bread / milk / apple`.
2. Future matching: `kaffe ↔ coffee`, `vand ↔ water`, `mælk ↔ milk`, `drikker ↔ drinks/is drinking`.
3. Reading choice: “Who drinks water?” → **the speaker (`jeg`)**; answer evidence is sentence two.
4. Constrained tiles, only after present-form teaching: `Nora / drikker / mælk` →
   **`Nora drikker mælk.`** No article tile is offered.

Exposure rule: the new mass bundle `mælk` and its article-free drink frame must be exposed before the
tile slot. Reused P1 entries still need P2 frame exposure; P1 completion is not inferred globally.

### Session 3 — Getting attention and receiving a roll (`4`: P2-09–P2-12)

**Real-life outcome:** recognize a shop and roll, politely get attention, and understand a handover.
Teach `undskyld` in an English attention-getting scene, `værsgo` in an English item-handover scene,
`en bolle` and `en butik`. The roll sense is distinct from the unrelated verb.

Reading and supplied English stage directions:

> Det er en butik.  
> Nora spiser en bolle.  
> [A customer politely gets the shopkeeper’s attention:] Undskyld.  
> [The shopkeeper hands over a roll:] Værsgo.

Planned slots:

1. Four-pair matching: `undskyld ↔ excuse me (getting attention)`, `værsgo ↔ here you are
   (handing something over)`, `en bolle ↔ a bread roll`, `en butik ↔ a shop`.
2. Context choice: in the attention-getting scene choose **`Undskyld.`** or `Værsgo.` Both are
   introduced functional alternatives; neither is dismissed as bad Danish.
3. Reading choice: “What does Nora eat?” → **a bread roll**, with **bread** as a reviewed food
   alternative. The roll is the only fact asserted for Nora.
4. Context choice: in the handover scene choose **`Værsgo.`** or `Undskyld.`

Exposure rule: scenes and full English translations establish the selected pragmatic meanings.
No silent verb coordination, imperative construction, gratitude response or taxonomic filler is added.

### Session 4 — Buying and paying (`3`: P2-13–P2-15)

**Real-life outcome:** read a minimal café purchase sequence and identify purchase versus payment.

Teaching presents `en café`, `at købe → køber`, and `at betale → betaler`. The location phrase is
supplied intact and does not teach a productive building-preposition rule.

Reading:

> Jeg køber en bolle i en café.  
> Jeg betaler.

Planned slots:

1. Meaning choice: `køber` → **buys**, contrasted with already introduced `spiser/drikker` and new
   `betaler`; answer `buys`.
2. Reading sequence: “What happens last?” → **the speaker pays**.
3. Constrained tiles after both verbs are taught: `Jeg / køber / en bolle` →
   **`Jeg køber en bolle.`** A separate supplied reading card may show `Jeg betaler.`; no price or
   payment method is elicited.

Exposure rule: the intransitive payment event is taught as part of this two-step situation. It must not
be offered as context-free English→Danish recall before that exposure.

### Session 5 — Marking a polite wish (`3`: P2-16–P2-18)

**Real-life outcome:** understand and assemble one polite purchase wish using already known products.

Teaching presents standalone/final `tak`, `gerne` in the licensed wish frame, and reused
`at ville → vil`. It revisits the modal rule: finite `vil` is followed by bare `købe`.

Reading:

> Jeg vil gerne købe en bolle.  
> Tak.

Planned slots:

1. Recognition in context: `gerne` marks the speaker's **willing wish/polite preference**, not a
   future-time label.
2. Constrained tiles: `Jeg / vil / gerne / købe / en bolle` →
   **`Jeg vil gerne købe en bolle.`** The only accepted order is the expressly taught frame.
3. Context choice: after a completed small purchase, choose **`Tak.`** from `Undskyld. / Tak. /
   Værsgo.` All are visibly reviewed functional alternatives; punctuation and capitalization
   are display support, not separate targets.

Exposure rule: the complete `vil gerne + bare infinitive` worked example is prerequisite to production.
Knowing `vil` from S11 does not prove exposure to `gerne` or this request-adjacent frame.

### Session 6 — Requesting a supplied quantity (`3`: P2-19–P2-21)

**Real-life outcome:** read two parallel café requests and assemble one constrained request for a cup or
bottle of an introduced drink.

Teaching presents request-frame bare `have`, `en kop`, and `en flaske`, then supplies the complete
quantity constituents `en kop kaffe` and `en flaske vand`. It explicitly contrasts request
`have` with the already stored possession use without asking the learner to produce `har`.

Reading:

> Jeg vil gerne have en kop kaffe, tak.  
> Nora vil gerne have en flaske vand.

Planned slots:

1. Quantity matching: `en kop kaffe ↔ a cup of coffee`; `en flaske vand ↔ a bottle of water`.
   English `of` does not create a Danish `af` tile.
2. Reading choice: “Who requests water?” → **Nora**.
3. Final constrained transfer tiles: `Jeg / vil / gerne / have / en kop kaffe / tak` →
   **`Jeg vil gerne have en kop kaffe, tak.`** The quantity constituent remains intact; punctuation is
   rendered, not assessed.
4. Planned alternate after its own exposure: `Jeg vil gerne have en flaske vand, tak.` The answer
   does not license arbitrary container/product combinations.

Exposure rule: final production requires saved exposure to the request sense of `have` and the exact
quantity constituent used. A known possession sense, a known beverage, or an open lesson is
insufficient. Unknown/out-of-scope learner text must not be converted into a claim of bad Danish.

## Source method and review limits

On 11 November 2026 each new candidate was looked up first through the official encoded lemma service
`https://ordregister.dk/lemma/COR/json/<encoded-lemma>`. For the original candidate batch, the selected lemma identity was then checked
through `https://ordregister.dk/id/COR.<lemma-id>.json`; the exact normalized active form IDs returned
are recorded row by row above. The same two-step check was performed for current reuse rows
`spise/æble/ville`; P1's recorded checks own `drikke/kaffe/vand`. The initially attempted path
`/id/COR.<id>/json` returned HTTP 404; the official [COR web-service documentation](https://ordregister.dk/doc/COR.Webservices.html)
confirmed the `.json` suffix used above. No bulk resource was downloaded or imported.

DDO pages linked above were consulted as reference locators for candidate meanings, homographs and
frames; dictionary wording and examples were not copied into course content. COR morphology does not
verify the authored English glosses, substance/count selections, request interpretation, valency or
sentence naturalness. DDO consultation likewise does not provide a content licence, frequency claim or
teaching approval. The six readings and exercise examples are original drafts.

Before a proposed private-preview activation, complete bounded source/content review and an exact
runtime contract for stable sense IDs, old fingerprints/pairs, direct-entry teaching, saved exposure
and supported operations. Danish specialist validation remains pending BL-022, including request
naturalness, quantity combinations and complete-beginner suitability; it is not a new mandatory
human-approval gate for draft preview work. P2 runtime activation remains unselected.
