Every count noun is first shown and subsequently labelled as its article+noun bundle:
`en bog`, `en avis`, `et brev`, `en computer`, `et kursus`. The articles are part of teaching
and matching surfaces, not a retroactive change to E008's noun-only answer contract and not a
new article+noun recall assessment. Q27 remains open. The selected mass uses are exactly bare

the chunks `i dag`, `nu`, `i + CITY`, `hjemme`, and `om morgenen` likewise use their own
answer shapes.

Only these proposition frames are authored:

- human subject + `arbejder`, with no complement and at most one of `i dag`, `i Aarhus`,
  `i Odense`, `hjemme`, or `om morgenen`;
- human subject + `læser` + `en bog/en avis/et brev`, with at most one of
  `i dag` or `nu`;
- human subject + `skriver et brev` only;
- human subject + intransitive `studerer`, with at most one of `nu`, `hjemme`, or
  `om morgenen`;
- human subject + `drikker kaffe/vand` only;
- existing possession `har en computer` and existing fixed identification
  `Det er et kursus`.

`Anna` and `Nora` are the packet's two supplied fictional human-subject placeholders. Their
use with P1 action frames is a new explicit packet permission awaiting review; their appearance
in earlier naming/copular material does not license them automatically as generic subjects.

Bare `dansk` is introduced on its own meaning card and matching row; P1 does not assess or
generate `læse dansk`, whose reading/study interpretation needs a more explicit context.
The `skrive`, `studere`, `drikke`, `hjemme`, `om morgenen`, computer and course permissions
are proposals awaiting content review and versioned implementation. Sharing a spelling or
theme does not broaden an old frame. In particular, do not generate `læse computer`,
`læse/studere et kursus`, `have et kursus`, `skrive en bog/en avis`, new `arbejde med/på/for`
complements, directional `hjem`, or productive `om + DAY-PART` substitutions. Treat
`om morgenen` as one supplied constituent; its definite component is not a grammar lesson.
No possessive determiner, definite noun outside that fixed chunk, new object pronoun or new
verb complement is introduced by any prompt.

## Shared session and grading contract

Each session starts with its required teaching card even on direct entry. A prior completed
session can justify a shorter review card only after a future plan records the relevant
visible-form and selected-sense exposures; a prerequisite label alone is not evidence of
learning. Opening a card that contains an answer, reading a text, viewing feedback or revealing
a solution records possible exposure to each visible answer form. Character entry help alone
is not language exposure.

The scripts deliberately separate introduction from assessment: session 1 uses recognition;
session 2 adds matching; sessions 3–4 use matching and closed tiles before typed form recall;
session 5 reviews the packet through reading and constrained production. A slot assesses one
primary target; a matching board is one activity containing one primary lexical assessment
per pair, with separate pair results. Other words, forms, order and meanings are either already taught, reviewed or
supplied. Deterministic shuffling may vary option position, column order and tile order, but
not the authored cue, selected sense, proposition, accepted answer or distractor rationale.

Feedback keeps target satisfaction, requested meaning, response constraint and bounded
language certainty separate:

| Case | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Exact selected answer within the stated shape | Target and meaning met; constraint met; acceptable within this packet. Green success requires all applicable facets. |
| Another displayed/introduced item | Target not met, but acknowledge the valid introduced meaning or form. Do not call it bad Danish. |
| Grammatical licensed sentence that misses an explicit order or meaning instruction | Language acceptable and off-target; explain the missed instruction. Do not relabel it ungrammatical. |
| Complete response matching a specifically reviewed corruption | Use a bounded known-issue explanation, such as infinitive instead of requested present or a synthetic article before the selected mass use. Do not generalize the diagnosis. |
| Unintroduced synonym, inflection, frame, extra prose or unsupported construction | Unknown unless a response-shape rule alone is decidable. Preserve raw input, do not silently map it to the target and do not claim invalid Danish. |
| A displayed alternative that also satisfies cue, meaning and constraints | Accept it explicitly or rewrite the item before publication. Never hide a legitimate answer behind one expected ID. |
| Unknown/duplicate supplied IDs or a response outside a closed choice bank | Malformed request after ownership checks, not a learner language error; persist no attempt. |

For the four new typed slots, retain raw input, apply NFC and trim surrounding whitespace,
then compare isolated words with `Locale.ROOT` case folding as selected in the activation contract.
Do not fuzzy-match Danish letters or accept a correct prefix with trailing material.
E008's existing case behavior remains unchanged. Sentence punctuation is supplied outside
tile banks. Each visibly identical tile occurrence has its own ID.

## Session 1 — Work, reading and today

**Outcome.** Recognize the four introduced meanings and identify a stated action and object in
a two-sentence routine.

**Prerequisite ownership.** The session itself owns all four P1 meanings. It may reuse the
already published human subjects `Anna`/`hun` and subject-first present clause as supplied
support, but it does not infer that the learner completed another course profile. Direct entry
briefly explains that `hun` means “she” and that the stored present form is the form used in
the reading; neither is a P1 lexical target.

**Required teaching.** Show four separate cards, then a combined review:

- `at arbejde → arbejder` — “to work → works/is working”; in P1 it means a person does work,
  with no `med/på/for` complement;
- `at læse → læser` — “to read → reads/is reading”; here it takes introduced written material;
- `en bog` — “a book”; always teach the common-gender count noun as this bundle;
- `i dag` — “today, the current calendar day”; the two words form one time constituent.

Annotated examples are `Anna arbejder i dag.` (“Anna works today.”) and
`Hun læser en bog.` (“She reads a book.”). They are teaching exposures and therefore are not
reused as hidden unseen-recall claims.

**Readable text.** `Anna arbejder i dag. Hun læser en bog.`

| Slot | Operation and exact material | Answer and distractor contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | Danish→English recognition. Cue `arbejder`; choices `works / reads / writes / drinks`. | `works`. Every alternative is an action meaning; English-only alternatives do not expose their later Danish forms. |
| 1.2 | Danish→English recognition. Cue `en bog`; choices `a book / a newspaper / a written letter / a computer`. | `a book`. All alternatives are count-noun meanings; the Danish surface retains its bundle. |
| 1.3 | Danish→English recognition. Cue `i dag`; choices `today / now / in the morning / this evening`. | `today`. All alternatives express time; later Danish equivalents are not shown or counted as introduced. |
| 1.4 | Reading comprehension over the exact two-sentence text. Cue “What does Anna read?” Choices `a book / a newspaper / a written letter`. | `a book`. The English-only distractors do not introduce the later Danish forms. |

**Exposure and feedback boundaries.** The reading exposes `arbejder`, `i dag`, `læser` and
`en bog` in propositions. A wrong displayed English meaning is known and off-target. An answer
outside a supplied choice is malformed for these closed slots, not unknown Danish. Do not
claim that the learner can produce the verbs or noun after recognition alone.

## Session 2 — Newspaper, letter, writing and computer

**Outcome.** Match four new article/form bundles to their meanings and recover explicit facts
from a short work-study text.

**Prerequisite ownership.** Session 1 forms may appear only after a visible review of
`læser/en bog`; the new session owns P1-05–P1-08. Existing `hun` and possession `har` are
supplied support. `har` is not a P1 target and does not license other computer actions.

**Required teaching.** Teach:

- `en avis` — “a newspaper”, the written-publication sense, not a company;
- `et brev` — “a letter, a written message”, not an alphabet character;
- `at skrive → skriver` — “to write → writes/is writing”; P1 permits only `skriver et brev`;
- `en computer` — “a computer device”; P1 permits possession/identification only.

Teach the complete examples `Nora skriver et brev.` (“Nora writes a letter.”),
`Hun har en computer.` (“She has a computer.”) and the reviewed sentence
`Hun læser en avis.` (“She reads a newspaper.”). Article+noun surfaces are teaching bundles;
no typed bundle recall is introduced.

**Readable text.** `Nora skriver et brev. Hun har en computer. Hun læser en avis.`

| Slot | Operation and exact material | Answer and distractor contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1 | Four-pair bilingual matching: `a newspaper ↔ en avis`, `a written letter ↔ et brev`, `writes ↔ skriver`, `a computer ↔ en computer`. | All four listed pairs; both columns shuffle independently. |
| 2.2 | English→Danish closed choice. Cue “Choose the introduced bundle for **a newspaper**.” Options `en bog / en avis / et brev / en computer`. | `en avis`. Every distractor is an introduced count-noun bundle with a distinct selected meaning. |
| 2.3 | Reading comprehension. Cue “What does Nora write?” Choices `a written letter / a newspaper / a book / on a computer`. | `a written letter`. “On a computer” is not licensed by the text or by P1's computer frame. |
| 2.4 | Reading comprehension. Cue “What does the text explicitly say Nora has?” Choices `a computer / a newspaper / a book`. | `a computer`. All choices name ownable items; the answer follows the stated possession fact, without inferring what else Nora owns. |

**Exposure and feedback boundaries.** The reading exposes complete `skriver et brev`,
`har en computer` and `læser en avis` propositions. Matching `skriver` to “reads” is a known
displayed-meaning error, not an invalid-form diagnosis. A learner statement such as
`Nora skriver en bog` or `Nora læser computer` is outside the licensed frames and remains
unknown; do not “correct” it through a theme-wide verb/object substitution rule.

## Session 3 — Studying, a course, Danish and now

**Outcome.** Recognize the selected education, course and language meanings, read one present
activity, assemble a licensed reading clause, and recall an exposed verb form or time form.

**Prerequisite ownership.** The session owns P1-09–P1-12 and visibly reviews `læser` plus
subject-first order. The subject `hun` is supplied support.
The course noun is not made the object of `studere`, `læse` or `have`. Typed slots follow the
teaching, matching and reading exposures in this session; they are recall after exposure.

**Required teaching.** Teach:

- `at studere → studerer` — “to be studying / pursue a course of education → is studying”,
  a learner activity used intransitively in P1; do not present bare English “study” as a
  contrast with `læse`;
- `et kursus` — “a course/class programme”, taught as a neuter count-noun bundle and used only
  on its own labelled meaning card; no unanchored identifying sentence is needed in the story;
- bare `dansk` — “Danish, the language”, without an indefinite article in the licensed
  meaning card and matching row, without introducing a reading/study complement contrast;
- `nu` — “now, at the current time”, not a discourse particle or conjunction.

Teach `Nora studerer.` (“Nora studies as a student.”) and
`Hun læser en bog nu.` (“She is reading a book now.”). Teach `et kursus` and `dansk` on
separate labelled cards. Their meanings are assessed by matching; this reading supplies no
claim about what course Nora takes or what language she studies.

**Readable text.** `Nora studerer. Hun læser en bog nu.`

| Slot | Operation and exact material | Answer and distractor contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | Four-pair bilingual matching: `studies (as a student) ↔ studerer`, `a course ↔ et kursus`, `Danish (language) ↔ dansk`, `now ↔ nu`. | All four listed pairs. The education gloss identifies the taught use; it does not declare other Danish translations invalid. `dansk` is bare and `et kursus` retains its bundle. |
| 3.2 | Reading comprehension. Cue “What does Nora read?” Choices `a book / a newspaper / a written letter`. | `a book`; the other two are introduced written-material meanings absent from this reading fact. |
| 3.3 | All-word tiles. Cue “Write **She reads a book now**. Begin with the subject.” Tiles `[hun] [læser] [en] [bog] [nu]`; period supplied. | `Hun læser en bog nu.` Each occurrence is used once. The specifically reviewed `Nu læser hun en bog.` preserves meaning but is acceptable-and-off-target because the cue requires the subject first; do not call it invalid Danish. |
| 3.4 | Typed selected-form recall. Cue “Type the introduced present form of **at studere**.” | `studerer`. `studere` is a known infinitive but misses the requested present-form target; another introduced present such as `læser` is acceptable Danish and off-target. An unrecognized form is unknown. |
| 3.5 | Typed chunk recall. Cue “Type the introduced Danish expression for **now**.” | `nu`. Extra words fail the one-expression constraint; an unsupported synonym remains unknown. |

**Exposure and feedback boundaries.** Slots 3.4–3.5 are not evidence of unseen recall. The
exact answer has appeared in the teaching, matching and reading. `Jeg studerer` would identify
the form but fail the one-word response constraint; do not accept a correct prefix. A response
`et dansk` is outside the selected language-noun use; give bounded feedback that this packet
teaches bare `dansk`, without making a language-wide claim.

## Session 4 — Coffee and water

**Outcome.** Understand the selected beverage frame, distinguish the two mass-noun objects,
assemble one complete clause and recall the exposed present or mass-noun form.

**Prerequisite ownership.** Session 4 owns P1-13–P1-15. It visibly reviews a human subject and
subject-first order. It does not require or assess an article choice; the absence of an article
is part of the selected mass-use teaching.

**Required teaching.** Teach:

- `at drikke → drikker` — “to drink → drinks/is drinking”; P1 permits only beverage objects
  `kaffe` and `vand`;
- bare `kaffe` — “coffee” as a beverage/substance in `drikker kaffe`;
- bare `vand` — “water” as liquid in `drikker vand`, excluding a bottle-serving and bodies of
  water.

Explicitly contrast the teaching shapes `en bog` but `kaffe` and `vand`: the contrast is
about these selected uses, not a rule that the lemmas can never occur in other constructions.

**Readable text.** `Anna drikker kaffe. Nora drikker vand.`

| Slot | Operation and exact material | Answer and distractor contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.1 | Three-pair bilingual matching: `drinks ↔ drikker`, `coffee ↔ kaffe`, `water ↔ vand`. | All three listed pairs; no synthetic articles appear. |
| 4.2 | Reading comprehension. Cue “What does Nora drink?” Choices `water / coffee`. | `water`. Both are plausible beverage meanings in this passage; do not pad the bank with an unrelated non-beverage. |
| 4.3 | Spare-word tiles. Cue “Write **Nora drinks water**. Begin with Nora. One word is extra.” Tiles `[Nora] [drikker] [drikke] [vand]`; period supplied. | `Nora drikker vand.`; leave `drikke`. `Nora drikke vand.` is a complete known infinitive-for-present issue. Using both verbs fails the response constraint and is otherwise unknown as a complete sentence. |
| 4.4 | Typed selected-form recall. Cue “Type the introduced present form of **at drikke**.” | `drikker`. `drikke` is known but off-target for the requested form; another introduced verb is valid vocabulary but wrong meaning. |
| 4.5 | Typed mass-noun recall. Cue “Type the introduced Danish word for **water as liquid**.” | `vand`. `kaffe` is known but wrong meaning. `en vand`/`et vand` fails the one-word shape and does not match the packet's selected mass use; do not claim that every Danish use with an article is impossible. |

**Exposure and feedback boundaries.** The reading and tile bank expose both `drikker` and
`drikke`, so the typed present task is recall after visible contrast. A response such as
`Anna drikker te` uses an unintroduced object and is unknown rather than invalid Danish.
Do not infer an alcohol sense, intransitive `drikke` or arbitrary food/drink valency.

## Session 5 — Place, home and the morning

**Outcome.** Read three bounded routine locations/times, distinguish their meanings, and
construct one constrained present-tense statement using earlier P1 material.

**Prerequisite ownership.** Session 5 owns P1-16–P1-18 and provides a visible packet review of
`arbejder`, `studerer`, subject-first order and the selected city names. It does not assume that
the learner completed S12, S14 or any general Danish preposition lesson. The city expression,
static `hjemme` and day-part chunk are supplied whole; they are not productive paradigms.

**Required teaching.** Teach:

- `i Aarhus` / `i Odense` — “in Aarhus / in Odense”, a supplied static city-location chunk;
- `hjemme` — “at home”, static location, without a productive contrast with directional
  `hjem`;
- `om morgenen` — “in the morning”, one habitual/day-part constituent whose internal
  definite noun and `om` construction are not analyzed here.

Teach only the reviewed-proposal examples `Anna arbejder i Aarhus.` (“Anna works in Aarhus.”),
`Nora studerer hjemme.` (“Nora studies at home.”) and
`Nora arbejder om morgenen.` (“Nora works in the morning.”). Each clause has one circumstance;
do not combine the place and time chunks in this packet.

**Readable text.** `Anna arbejder i Aarhus. Nora studerer hjemme. Nora arbejder om morgenen.`

| Slot | Operation and exact material | Answer and distractor contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 | Three-pair bilingual matching: `in Aarhus ↔ i Aarhus`, `at home ↔ hjemme`, `in the morning ↔ om morgenen`. | All three listed pairs. Treat each Danish surface as one pedagogical unit even if tiles later need component occurrences. |
| 5.2 | Reading comprehension. Cue “Where does Nora study?” Choices `at home / in Aarhus / in Odense`. | `at home`. The city alternatives are valid introduced place chunks but are not stated. |
| 5.3 | Reading comprehension. Cue “When does Nora work?” Choices `in the morning / today / now`. | `in the morning`. The named subject links the question to exactly one explicit time fact. |
| 5.4 | All-word tiles, packet transfer. Cue “Write **I work at home**. Begin with the subject.” Tiles `[jeg] [arbejder] [hjemme]`; period supplied. | `Jeg arbejder hjemme.` The response reviews `arbejder` with the newly introduced static location. No alternative ordering satisfies the explicit subject-first cue in this three-constituent bank. |
| 5.5 | All-word tiles, changed city. Cue “Write **I work in Odense**. Begin with the subject.” Tiles `[jeg] [arbejder] [i] [Odense]`; period supplied. | `Jeg arbejder i Odense.` Use both `i` and `Odense` once. `I Odense arbejder jeg.` is grammatical within the separately reviewed fronted family but acceptable-and-off-target here because the cue requires subject first; it must not be generated until that exact frame is active. |

**Exposure and feedback boundaries.** Slot 5.4 is constrained transfer because the exact
first-person proposition is absent from the teaching text, but every sense and form is already
exposed. Slot 5.5 changes the named city within the supplied two-city allowlist; it is not
evidence of productive `i + any place`. `hjem` is an unsupported directional form here and
receives unknown feedback. `morgenen` alone misses the taught whole chunk; explain the missing
chunk shape without declaring the standalone noun invalid Danish. A response with two
circumstances, such as `Jeg arbejder hjemme om morgenen`, remains unknown until that exact
combination and ordering receive separate frame review.
