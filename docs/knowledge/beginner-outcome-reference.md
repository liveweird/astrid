## Primary reference points

The Council of Europe's **2020 CEFR Companion volume**, sections 3.1.1.3, 3.2.1.2 and 3.3.1.2, distinguishes reading, written production and written interaction. Its A1 reading scale concerns very short texts with familiar language and opportunities to reread. A1 written production includes matters of personal relevance and isolated phrases/sentences; A1 written interaction includes asking for or passing on personal details. Pre-A1 includes basic personal information and more limited activities, sometimes with pictures or dictionary support. These are activity descriptions, not a Danish grammar sequence or fixed vocabulary quota. [Official volume, printed pp. 54, 66 and 82–84](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4); [edition and language versions](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-companion-volume-and-its-language-versions).

SIRI describes DU1, DU2 and DU3 as different education tracks, with different schooling/literacy backgrounds and expected acquisition pace. They are not three successive beginner proficiency levels. English-speaking identity alone does not select a track. [Official track descriptions](https://danskogproever.dk/sprogcenter/om-danskuddannelserne/danskuddannelserne/).

The **2026 module-testing guide** covers oral and written assessment, with some modules testing only oral communication. Its module overview and proficiency model therefore cannot be copied as a reading/writing-only progression. Published guidance is a reference; confidential live test material is not a content source for Astrid. [SIRI guide, printed pp. 3–4 and appendices 2/4](https://danskogproever.dk/media/r5lnqr1y/vejledning-til-modultestning-2026.pdf).

## Proposed course-authoring record

For each future everyday outcome, record:

1. **Purpose and situation:** what the learner needs to understand or communicate, with an original text/context.
2. **Observable response:** identify information, provide a short message, or another bounded action; distinguish comprehension from form selection.
3. **Language dependencies:** selected G/C/L entries and senses, including contextual vocabulary supplied rather than assessed.
4. **Support conditions:** examples, glossary/dictionary, hints and response scaffolding; preserve them with the evidence.
5. **Success and uncertainty:** meaning conveyed, the grammar target, acceptable alternatives and what the grader cannot judge.
6. **Transfer check:** a separately authored unseen example, with delayed checking considered later; no scheduler or numerical mastery rule is selected.

Start with one agreed outcome and reviewed cases after playground feedback. Danish teaching review should examine the proposed order, naturalness and support conditions. Learner trials must then examine comprehensibility and transfer; a source citation or passing software test cannot supply that evidence.
