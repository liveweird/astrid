# Danish Grammar Curriculum Map

## Purpose and status

This working draft proposes a grammar sequence for English-speaking complete beginners who initially learn through reading and writing. It maps 58 small skills from first word and sentence recognition to advanced written structures. The phases are pedagogical proposals, not official CEFR levels, and the map is not a claim that every Danish construction, register, or exception has been covered.

The grammatical claims below were checked against the sources listed at the end on 6 November 2026. The sequence, skill boundaries, examples, and production expectations still need review by a teacher experienced in Danish as an additional language. No such review has yet taken place. Spoken pronunciation, listening, conversation management, dialects, and detailed colloquial syntax are outside this reading-and-writing draft.

A [source-backed follow-up review](foundation-source-review.md) on 7 November corrected later draft scope, case, notation and support-skill gaps. Source-backed linguistic statements and proposed curriculum dependencies remain distinct; the active beginner slice is unchanged.

## How to read a skill record

Each stable `G` identifier names one assessable target. Identifiers must not be reused if a target is later retired. A complete content record derived from this map should contain:

- the identifier, title, proposed phase, learning objective, and prerequisite identifiers;
- a learner explanation, including a relevant English–Danish contrast;
- recognized forms and structures, forms the learner must produce, original correct examples, likely errors, and grading criteria;
- important exceptions and deliberately deferred variants;
- links to compatible sentence constructions and vocabulary entries;
- source references, authoring status, linguistic-review status, and engine-support status.

In the compact records below, **R** means the learner should recognize and interpret the form; **P** means the learner should produce it in constrained writing. “Later” marks variants that should not be treated as errors outside the current target. Prerequisites express the minimum dependency graph, not every useful review connection.

## Proposed first connected slice

The initial slice is **G001–G006**. It supports these deliberately narrow patterns:

- `C001`: *Jeg sover.*
- `C002`: *Jeg har en bog. Hun læser en avis. Vi spiser et æble.*
- `C003`: *Det er en bog. Det er et hus.* Here *det* is the grammatical subject in a fixed identification pattern.

The slice tests word boundaries, a few word classes and sentence roles, subject pronouns, present finite verbs, subject-first statements, and `en`/`et` gender. It does not yet ask learners to produce generic professions with bare predicate nouns, adjective agreement, plural noun phrases, tense contrasts, or free word order. Infinitive and past forms may exist as vocabulary metadata, but vague temporal cues must not be used to test tense before the later tense skills.

## Phase 1 First words and clauses

### G001 Orthography and word units

**Prerequisites:** none. **Objective:** recognize Danish letters `æ`, `ø`, and `å`, spaces, sentence-initial capitals, and final punctuation; treat a written word as a unit without assuming English spacing rules. Contrast: Danish compounds can later be one orthographic word where English uses several. Example: *En blå bil.* **R/P:** segment and copy short strings accurately. **Later:** compound formation and full capitalization rules. [S1, S9]

### G002 Word classes and basic sentence roles

**Prerequisites:** G001. **Objective:** distinguish common examples of noun, article, pronoun, verb, and adjective, and distinguish a word class from its function as subject, finite verb, or object. Contrast: English labels transfer imperfectly; position alone does not determine Danish word class. Example: in *Hun læser bogen*, *hun* is a pronoun and subject. **R/P:** label supplied words and roles; no free analysis yet. **Later:** the remaining word classes, phrases, predicatives, indirect objects, and clauses as constituents. [S1, S2]

### G003 Subject pronouns

**Prerequisites:** G002. **Objective:** select and interpret `jeg`, `du`, `han`, `hun`, `vi`, `I`, and plural `de` as human subjects; permit fixed identifying `det` only in *Det er en bog* and equivalent C003 statements. Contrast: English `I` is capitalized, Danish *jeg* is not; Danish `I` “you plural” is capitalized. **R/P:** choose one of these seven human subjects from explicit context or use the fixed identity pattern. **Later:** `den` and general referential `det` in G038, object case, impersonal *det*, and formal *De*. [S5]

### G004 Finite present verbs

**Prerequisites:** G002. **Objective:** distinguish infinitive and finite present forms and use a small reviewed present-tense set, including irregular *er* and *har*. Danish finite verbs do not agree with person or number: *jeg sover*, *vi sover*. Contrast: English changes *sleep/sleeps*. **R/P:** supply the licensed present form after a subject. **Later:** imperatives, modals, tense choice, and productive inflection classes; past forms are metadata only here. [S4]

### G005 Simple subject-first statements

**Prerequisites:** G003, G004. **Objective:** build a main-clause statement with subject before the finite verb, optionally followed by a licensed complement: *Jeg sover. Hun læser en avis. Det er et hus.* Contrast: Danish later requires finite-verb-second order when another constituent is first. **R/P:** order only reviewed subject-first patterns. **Later:** negation, questions, fronting, generic professions, and freely selected complements. [S2, S3]

### G006 Noun gender and indefinite singular articles

**Prerequisites:** G001, G002. **Objective:** learn each count noun with common gender `en` or neuter `et` and form an indefinite singular noun phrase: *en bog*, *et æble*. Contrast: English `a/an` follows sound; Danish article choice encodes lexical gender and usually must be learned with the noun. **R/P:** select the stored article for licensed nouns. **Later:** article omission, mass nouns, plurals, and variation in noun gender. [S3]

## Phase 2 Building noun phrases

### G007 Direct objects

**Prerequisites:** G005, G006. **Objective:** recognize and produce a noun phrase as the direct object of a licensed transitive verb: *Vi køber en stol.* Contrast: English and Danish both commonly use subject–verb–object, but verb meanings determine whether an object is permitted. **R/P:** identify and place one reviewed object; reject incompatible verb frames. **Later:** object pronouns, indirect objects, particles, and object clauses. [S2]

### G008 Definite singular noun suffixes

**Prerequisites:** G006. **Objective:** contrast indefinite *en bog/et hus* with suffixed definite *bogen/huset*. Danish normally attaches the simple definite article to the noun, unlike English’s separate `the`. **R/P:** select and form reviewed `-(e)n` and `-(e)t` nouns. **Later:** irregular stem changes, names, possessors, and preposed definite articles with adjectives. [S1, S3]

### G009 Indefinite noun plurals

**Prerequisites:** G006. **Objective:** use stored plural forms and recognize common `-er`, `-r`, `-e`, and zero patterns: *en dag – dage*, *et æble – æbler*. Contrast: Danish plural cannot be predicted from gender alone. **R/P:** retrieve the reviewed plural rather than infer an unrestricted rule. **Later:** irregular and borrowed plurals. [S3]

### G010 Definite noun plurals

**Prerequisites:** G008, G009. **Objective:** form and interpret definite plurals with stored `-ne/-ene` patterns: *dagene*, *husene*. Contrast: definiteness is again suffixed rather than expressed by English `the`. **R/P:** transform reviewed indefinite plurals to definite plurals. **Later:** stem alternations and competing accepted forms. [S3]

### G011 Basic adjective use with common-gender nouns

**Prerequisites:** G006. **Objective:** put a basic adjective before an indefinite common-gender noun: *en grøn cykel*. Contrast: Danish adjectives later agree with gender, number, and definiteness; English adjectives do not. **R/P:** use reviewed regular adjectives only in this attributive pattern. **Later:** predicative adjectives, `-t`, `-e`, comparison, and invariant adjectives. [S6]

### G012 Adjective agreement in neuter singular

**Prerequisites:** G011. **Objective:** add the licensed neuter `-t` form before an indefinite neuter noun: *et grønt æble*. **R/P:** choose common versus neuter form from the noun in this attributive pattern. **Later:** predicative adjectives, spelling changes, vowel-final adjectives, and adjectives whose form is unchanged. [S6]

### G013 Adjectives in plural and definite noun phrases

**Prerequisites:** G008, G009, G011, G012. **Objective:** use the `-e` form in regular plural and definite adjective phrases: *grønne cykler; den grønne cykel; det grønne hus*. In the definite singular pattern, Danish uses preposed `den/det`, adjective `-e`, and a noun without its suffixed definite article: *den grønne cykel*, not `den grønne cyklen`. Contrast: English has no adjective agreement. **R/P:** produce reviewed regular phrases. **Later:** lexicalized and exceptional adjective forms. [S6]

### G014 Possessive determiners and agreement

**Prerequisites:** G003, G006, G009. **Objective:** use *min/mit/mine* and *din/dit/dine* before nouns, matching the possessed noun rather than the possessor: *mit hus, mine bøger*. Contrast: English `my/your` is invariant. In this ordinary preposed possessive pattern, the head noun has no definite suffix or additional article. **R/P:** select a form from explicit noun gender/number. **Later:** adjective combinations unless their G013 support is supplied, *sin*, third-person genitives, stressed possessives, and archaic/formal *vor*. [S5]

## Phase 3 Core clause operations

### G015 Genitive s and noun possessors

**Prerequisites:** G008. **Objective:** recognize and produce simple possessor + `s` phrases: *Marias cykel*, *hundens mad*. In these ordinary preposed genitive patterns, the head noun has neither a separate definite article nor a definite suffix. Contrast: Danish writes the genitive `s` without the English apostrophe. **R/P:** use simple names and nouns. **Later:** complex possessors, names ending in sibilants, and alternative *til* phrases. [S3]

### G016 Time, place, and manner adverbials

**Prerequisites:** G005. **Objective:** add reviewed adverbs and prepositional phrases after the clause core: *Vi arbejder hjemme i dag.* Contrast: Danish adverbial order is flexible but not free. **R/P:** interpret several adverbial meanings and produce one neutral final adverbial. **Later:** sentence adverbs, fronting, multiple-adverbial order, and adverbial `-t`. [S7]

### G017 Main-clause negation

**Prerequisites:** G005. **Objective:** place *ikke* after the finite verb in a neutral subject-first main clause: *Hun arbejder ikke.* Contrast: Danish does not require English-style `do` support. **R/P:** negate reviewed simple clauses without changing tense. **Later:** auxiliary verb complexes after G027 and G030, constituent negation, negative pronouns, subordinate-clause position, and focus scope. [S7, S8]

### G018 Yes or no questions

**Prerequisites:** G005. **Objective:** form a neutral question by putting the finite verb before the subject: *Læser du avisen?* Contrast: Danish uses inversion directly and not English `do`. **R/P:** transform reviewed subject-first statements and restore statement order. **Later:** question tags, echo questions, and modal nuance. [S2]

### G019 Wh questions

**Prerequisites:** G018. **Objective:** form questions with a reviewed wh constituent followed by finite verb and subject: *Hvor bor du? Hvad læser hun?* Contrast: English and Danish both front many wh forms, but Danish keeps its own verb-second pattern. **R/P:** select question word by requested role and order a simple question. **Later:** subject questions, preposition stranding, and embedded questions. [S5]

### G020 Imperatives

**Prerequisites:** G004. **Objective:** recognize and form reviewed imperatives, normally without an overt subject: *Læs bogen! Vent her!* Contrast: Danish forms often remove infinitive `-e`, but forms must be stored where spelling changes. **R/P:** produce short written instructions. **Later:** politeness, negated imperatives, coordinated commands, and reflexive objects. [S2, S4]

### G021 Fronting and finite verb second

**Prerequisites:** G016, G018. **Objective:** front one constituent and keep the finite verb second, placing the subject after it: *I dag arbejder vi hjemme.* Contrast: English normally keeps the subject before the verb after such an adverbial. **R/P:** reorder a supplied clause with one fronted adverbial. **Later:** complex fronted constituents, marked objects, and exceptions to declarative V2. [S8, S11, S12]

### G022 Subject predicatives and linking verbs

**Prerequisites:** G005, G011, G012. **Objective:** use licensed linking verbs with a subject predicative: *Huset er stort. Hun hedder Lea.* Contrast: the complement describes or identifies the subject rather than acting as an object. In reviewed singular descriptive frames, the adjective agrees with the subject’s grammatical gender: *Bilen er stor; Huset er stort*. Subjects may be supplied; their definite forms need not be independently produced here. **R/P:** distinguish object and predicative and select a reviewed singular adjective form. **Later:** plural predicatives, bare profession nouns, complex predicatives, and agreement edge cases. [S2, S6]

## Phase 4 Verb frames and time

### G023 Indirect objects and two-object frames

**Prerequisites:** G007. **Objective:** recognize recipient and theme in a licensed frame: *Hun giver barnet en bog.* Contrast: Danish word order and choice between two objects and a preposition depend on the verb and information structure. **R/P:** produce only reviewed recipient–theme frames. **Later:** pronoun order and alternations with *til*. [S2]

### G024 Prepositional complements

**Prerequisites:** G007, G016. **Objective:** learn a verb or adjective together with a required or conventional preposition: *Hun venter på bussen.* Contrast: English equivalents often select a different preposition, so literal translation is unreliable. **R/P:** choose a stored preposition in a licensed frame. **Later:** stranded prepositions, particles, and free versus selected adverbials. [S1, S7]

### G025 Coordination of words, phrases, and main clauses

**Prerequisites:** G005, G007. **Objective:** coordinate equivalent units with *og, eller,* and *men*: *Hun læser og skriver; Jeg går, men han bliver.* **R/P:** identify what is shared and avoid joining unlike roles. **Later:** ellipsis, coordinated subordinate clauses, comma rules, and scope ambiguity. [S10]

### G026 Preterite

**Prerequisites:** G004, G005. **Objective:** use stored regular and high-frequency irregular past forms for completed or past-situated events: *I går arbejdede vi; Hun gik hjem.* Contrast: Danish verbs still do not agree with person. **R/P:** choose present versus preterite only with unambiguous context. **Later:** narrative viewpoint, modal past, and subtle present–past discourse choices. [S2, S4]

### G027 Present perfect

**Prerequisites:** G026. **Objective:** form *har* plus an invariant past participle in reviewed cases: *Jeg har læst bogen.* Contrast: Danish and English perfect usage overlaps but is not identical. **R/P:** assemble the form and interpret a clear result or prior event. **Later:** auxiliary *være*, temporal-adverb restrictions, and perfect versus preterite discourse choice. [S4]

### G028 Past perfect

**Prerequisites:** G027. **Objective:** form *havde* plus past participle to locate an event before a past reference point: *Hun havde spist, før vi kom.* **R/P:** order two explicitly timed events. **Later:** complex narrative sequencing and auxiliary *være*. [S2, S4]

### G029 Future reference

**Prerequisites:** G004, G005. **Objective:** recognize that Danish can express future time with present tense plus context and with modal constructions such as *vil* or *skal*: *Vi rejser i morgen.* Contrast: there is no single obligatory future inflection corresponding to English `will`. **R/P:** use a reviewed form for an explicit intended meaning. **Later:** prediction versus intention, obligation, scheduling, and modal ambiguity. [S2]

### G030 Modal verbs and bare infinitives

**Prerequisites:** G004, G005. **Objective:** use reviewed modal meanings with an infinitive without *at*: *Hun kan svømme; Vi skal gå.* Contrast: Danish modal meanings and English translations do not match one to one. **R/P:** select a modal from explicit ability, permission, necessity, or intention contexts. **Later:** epistemic readings, past modals, and ellipsis. [S4]

## Phase 5 Verb complexes and pronouns

### G031 Infinitives with at

**Prerequisites:** G004, G005. **Objective:** distinguish infinitive marker *at* from the subordinate conjunction *at* and use licensed verb + *at* + infinitive frames: *Hun prøver at læse.* **R/P:** build reviewed nonfinite complements. **Later:** control, raising, infinitive clauses, omitted *at*, and comma diagnostics. [S4, S10]

### G032 Active and passive

**Prerequisites:** G026, G027. **Objective:** contrast active clauses with reviewed *blive* passives and recognize morphological `-s` passives: *De maler huset; Huset bliver malet.* Contrast: Danish has both periphrastic and `-s` passive choices. **R/P:** produce a neutral *blive* passive and recognize common `-s` forms in reviewed simple transitive patient-subject patterns. **Later:** impersonal and other passive argument structures, state/event distinctions, tense range, agents with *af*, and stylistic choice. [S4]

### G033 Verb particles

**Prerequisites:** G007, G021. **Objective:** treat a verb plus particle as a lexical unit where required: *Hun tager jakken på.* Contrast: the English equivalent may be a single verb or place its particle differently. **R/P:** preserve the reviewed particle and neutral object placement. **Later:** pronoun placement, fronting, stress-dependent particle/adverb analyses, and separability variation. [S7]

### G034 Participles as verbal and adjectival forms

**Prerequisites:** G011, G013, G027. **Objective:** distinguish invariant verbal participles in reviewed *have*-perfect frames from inflected attributive participles in definite noun phrases: *De har lukket døren; den lukkede dør.* **R/P:** classify and complete these reviewed forms. **Later:** broader auxiliary and state/passive distinctions, lexicalized participles, and present participles in `-ende`. [S4, S6, S9]

### G035 Object pronouns

**Prerequisites:** G003, G007. **Objective:** use nominative subjects versus object forms such as *mig, dig, ham, hende, os, jer,* and *dem*: *Hun ser ham.* Contrast: Danish maintains a case contrast that English also shows in some pronouns, but the inventories differ. **R/P:** replace an explicit object noun phrase in a neutral clause. **Later:** unstressed placement, coordination case, and formal *Dem*. [S5]

### G036 Reflexive and reciprocal pronouns

**Prerequisites:** G035. **Objective:** use *sig* when a third-person object refers back to the clause subject and *hinanden* for reciprocal reference: *De vasker sig; De hjælper hinanden.* Contrast: Danish *sig* is invariant for third-person gender and number. **R/P:** resolve clear local reference. **Later:** long-distance and lexical reflexives, body-part constructions, and reciprocal ambiguity. [S5]

### G037 Reflexive possessive sin

**Prerequisites:** G014, G035, G036. **Objective:** in initial standard-Danish contexts with singular *han* or *hun* as subject, choose *sin/sit/sine* for that subject’s possession and *hans/hendes* for another possessor: *Hun tager sin bog.* The forms agree with the possessed noun. With plural *de*, the core pattern uses *deres*: *De tager deres bøger.* **R/P:** resolve unambiguous contexts with one local subject. **Later:** singular uses of *de*, *man*, nonhuman subjects, hidden clauses, coordinated or plural subjects, regional variation, and reported perspective. [S5, S15]

### G038 Den, det, de, and dem as reference forms

**Prerequisites:** G003, G008, G010, G035. **Objective:** for subject reference to things, select *den/det/de* by the antecedent’s grammatical gender and number: *Hvor er huset? Det er her.* Object and prepositional reference must also respect case: plural *dem*, following G035, as in *Jeg læser dem* referring to *bøgerne*. Singular *den/det* retain their form in these cases. Contrast: English largely uses singular `it`; Danish encodes noun class as well as the plural case distinction. **R/P:** resolve an explicit nearby antecedent. **Later:** human reference, discourse-deictic *det*, dummy subjects, and demonstrative stress. [S5]

## Phase 6 Determiners and subordinate clauses

### G039 Demonstratives

**Prerequisites:** G013, G038. **Objective:** use *denne/dette/disse* and recognize stressed *den/det/de* before a noun: *dette hus; disse bøger*. **R/P:** match gender and number in explicit contrastive contexts. **Later:** colloquial *den her/det her/de her*, distal contrasts, and standalone use. [S5]

### G040 Indefinite and negative pronouns

**Prerequisites:** G009, G017. **Objective:** use a bounded set of *nogen/noget/nogle* and *ingen/intet* in clear count, noncount, question, and negative contexts. Contrast: Danish form choice does not map mechanically to English `some/any/no`. **R/P:** first recognize all forms; produce only unambiguous reviewed contrasts. **Later:** polarity subtleties, free-choice readings, and regional/usage variation. [S5]

### G041 Numerals and quantifiers

**Prerequisites:** G009, G010. **Objective:** combine reviewed cardinal numbers and quantifiers with count or mass noun forms: *to bøger; meget vand; mange dage*. Contrast: English and Danish differ in individual quantifier selection and noun morphology. **R/P:** match countability and number in reviewed vocabulary. **Later:** measure phrases, fractions, distributives, ordinal syntax, and agreement variation. [S1]

### G042 Dummy det and existential der

**Prerequisites:** G005, G022, G038. **Objective:** distinguish referential *det* from nonreferential weather/time *det* and presentation with *der*: *Det regner; Der står en cykel udenfor.* Contrast: English also uses dummy `it/there`, but Danish placement follows Danish V2. **R/P:** recognize all; produce a small set of weather and existence patterns. **Later:** definiteness effects, extraposition, and presentational word-order variants. [S2, S8]

### G043 Recognizing subordinate clauses

**Prerequisites:** G005, G025. **Objective:** identify a finite clause functioning as a unit inside another clause and recognize common introducers such as *at, fordi, hvis,* and wh words. A subordinate clause cannot normally stand as an independent utterance. **R/P:** bracket a supplied clause and name its role; production remains formulaic. **Later:** omitted introducers and ambiguous introducer classes. [S8, S10]

### G044 Subordinate clause word order

**Prerequisites:** G017, G043. **Objective:** place a sentence adverb such as *ikke* between subject and finite verb in a neutral subordinate clause: *fordi hun ikke arbejder*. Contrast: the corresponding main clause is *hun arbejder ikke*. **R/P:** reorder reviewed subordinate clauses and diagnose the contrast. **Later:** embedded V2, marked information structure, and multiple adverbs. [S8, S11, S12]

### G045 Object clauses with at

**Prerequisites:** G007, G043, G044. **Objective:** use a finite *at* clause as the object of a licensed reporting or cognition verb: *Jeg ved, at hun kommer* or, under a no-start-comma policy, *Jeg ved at hun kommer*. Contrast: Danish *at* here is a conjunction, not infinitive `at`. **R/P:** produce one configured punctuation style consistently. **Later:** omitted *at*, factivity, tense perspective, and alternative comma policy. [S10, S13]

### G046 Adverbial subordinate clauses

**Prerequisites:** G016, G043, G044. **Objective:** use reviewed clauses of time, reason, and condition after the main clause: *Vi går hjem, hvis det regner.* **R/P:** select an introducer from explicit meaning and preserve subordinate order. **Later:** concessive, purpose, result, comparative, and reduced clauses; punctuation expands in G057. [S8, S10]

## Phase 7 Clause combinations and comparison

### G047 Fronted subordinate clauses and V2

**Prerequisites:** G021, G046. **Objective:** front a complete subordinate clause as the first constituent and invert the following main clause: *Hvis det regner, bliver vi hjemme.* Contrast: English does not invert the main clause in this pattern. **R/P:** transform an after-main-clause condition to initial position. **Later:** optional correlates such as *så* and complex clause boundaries. [S8, S10]

### G048 Indirect questions

**Prerequisites:** G019, G043, G044. **Objective:** embed a non-subject wh question or an *om* yes/no question with an explicit subject and subordinate order: *Jeg ved ikke, hvor hun bor; Han spørger, om du kommer.* Contrast: English learners may wrongly retain direct-question inversion. **R/P:** convert a supplied direct question into a licensed complement. **Later:** embedded subject questions and their *der* construction (also deferred in C021), tense perspective, politeness frames, and omitted elements. [S8, S10]

### G049 Relative clauses

**Prerequisites:** G008, G043, G044. **Objective:** attach a simple restrictive relative clause with *som* or subject *der*: *bogen som jeg læser; kvinden der bor her*. **R/P:** connect an explicit antecedent and identify the missing role. **Later:** object omission, prepositions, nonrestrictive relatives, *hvis*, complex antecedents, and comma policy. [S5, S10]

### G050 Adjective and adverb comparison

**Prerequisites:** G011, G012, G016. **Objective:** form and interpret reviewed comparative and superlative patterns, including `-ere/-est`, *mere/mest*, and stored irregular forms: *hurtig, hurtigere, hurtigst*. **R/P:** compare explicit entities with *end*. **Later:** attributive superlative agreement, equality with *så … som*, and lexical restrictions. [S6, S7]

### G051 Adverbial adjective forms in t

**Prerequisites:** G012, G016. **Objective:** distinguish an agreeing neuter adjective from an adjective used adverbially, often with `-t`: *et roligt barn; barnet sover roligt*. The word can remain an adjective by class while functioning adverbially. **R/P:** classify and form reviewed regular cases. **Later:** optional or forbidden `-t`, lexical adverbs, and meaning changes. [S1, S7]

### G052 Open and hypothetical conditions

**Prerequisites:** G026, G030, G046, G047. **Objective:** contrast an open condition with a reviewed hypothetical pattern: *Hvis det regner, bliver vi hjemme; Hvis jeg havde tid, ville jeg komme.* **R/P:** interpret both and produce scaffolded pairs. **Later:** strong counterfactual readings, mixed time reference, inversion without *hvis*, modal nuance, and pragmatic distancing. [S8, S10, S14]

## Phase 8 Advanced written structures

### G053 Reported statements and tense perspective

**Prerequisites:** G026, G028, G037, G045. **Objective:** report a prior statement while tracking speaker, pronoun reference, and time: *Hun sagde, at hun var træt.* Contrast: Danish tense backshift is meaning-sensitive rather than a purely mechanical replacement rule. **R/P:** rewrite only contexts with explicit time and reference. **Later:** free indirect discourse, retained present, reported commands, and evidential nuance. S16 supplies supplementary descriptive evidence; a stronger dedicated tense-perspective reference remains open. [S2, S10, S16]

### G054 Coordination, ellipsis, and scope

**Prerequisites:** G025, G043, G046. **Objective:** interpret coordinated clauses and shared material without assigning one subject to an unrelated finite verb: *Hun læser og skriver; Hun læser, og han skriver.* **R/P:** restore omitted parallel material and punctuate clear clause boundaries. **Later:** gapping, right-node sharing, attachment ambiguity, and coordination across unlike categories. [S10]

### G055 Complex noun phrases and nominalization

**Prerequisites:** G013, G015, G031, G049. **Objective:** unpack and build noun phrases with licensed determiners or possessors, adjectives, prepositional modifiers, or relative clauses: *Marias nye bog om København*. Recognize common deverbal nouns as nouns rather than finite clauses. **R/P:** expand one modifier at a time and identify the head noun, using only variants whose support skills have been introduced. A possessive-determiner variant additionally requires G014; regular adjective agreement uses G013. **Later:** heavy prenominal compounds, apposition, abstract style, and ambiguous attachment. [S1, S3]

### G056 Compounds and written word boundaries

**Prerequisites:** G001, G002, G009. **Objective:** recognize and write transparent Danish compounds as one word: *kaffekop, cykelhjelm*. Contrast: English often writes comparable expressions as two words. **R/P:** join only reviewed productive combinations and identify the semantic head. **Later:** linking elements, hyphens, proper-name compounds, stress-dependent distinctions, and lexicalized meanings. [S3, S9]

### G057 Written punctuation and capitalization

**Prerequisites:** G025, G043, G046, G049, G054. **Objective:** use sentence-final marks, capitals, list commas, commas between coordinated finite clauses, and one declared subordinate-clause comma policy. Danish permits writing with or without the optional start comma, but a text must apply its chosen system consistently. **R/P:** punctuate parsed, unambiguous sentences. **Later:** parentheticals, quotations, colon/semicolon, titles, and difficult clause boundaries. [S9, S10, S13]

### G058 Marked and advanced written word order

**Prerequisites:** G021, G044, G047. **Objective:** recognize documented marked variants, including negation before the subject in some *hvis* clauses: `hvis ikke S Vf`, not finite-verb–subject inversion. S11 examines TV debate and reports supplementary written-corpus occurrences. **R/P:** interpret source-annotated examples; production is limited to rewriting them into neutral order. These observations do not establish freely interchangeable neutral production patterns. **Later:** a fuller corpus-based inventory of embedded V2, extraposition, presentation, focus, and genre or register differences. G053/G055 are optional later review connections rather than hard prerequisites. [S11, S12]

## Dependency and coverage checks

The [coverage crosswalk](foundation-coverage.md) links every skill to bounded construction families or an explicit authoring/coverage gap. Only G004/G005/G006 are directly assessed by current formats; G001/G002/G003 are supporting pilot knowledge. Later links do not activate a construction or override its lexical and prerequisite restrictions.

The prerequisite graph runs only from lower to higher identifiers, so this draft contains no cycles. Every prerequisite named above exists. The phases preserve that partial order, although review and spaced practice should revisit earlier skills after their first introduction.

Coverage is intentionally broad rather than exhaustive. The map identifies the principal morphology, noun-phrase structure, verb complexes, clause types, word-order contrasts, reference systems, and writing conventions needed for later content design. It does not yet inventory every valency frame, particle verb, idiom, stylistic construction, punctuation edge case, or accepted variant. Those belong in the linked construction and vocabulary catalogues and should be promoted into new stable skills only when they support a distinct learning target.

## Source register

- **S1 — Word classes:** [Ordklasser, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/ordklasser/). Supports the distinction between word class, grammatical function, and inflection, and the official inventory used by Retskrivningsordbogen.
- **S2 — Terms and roles:** [Grammatiske ord, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/grammatiske-ord/). Supports standard terms and examples of subject, finite verb, objects, predicatives, adverbials, and tense forms.
- **S3 — Nouns:** [Substantiver, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/substantiver-navneord/). Supports common/neuter gender, number, definiteness, genitive `s`, and one-word noun compounds.
- **S4 — Verbs:** [Verber, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/verber-udsagnord/). Supports infinitive, imperative, present, preterite, passive, participles, and auxiliary constructions.
- **S5 — Pronouns:** [Pronomener, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/pronomener-stedord/). Supports subject/object/genitive forms, possessive agreement, *sig*, *sin*, demonstratives, interrogatives, relatives, and indefinite pronouns.
- **S6 — Adjectives:** [Adjektiver, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/adjektiver-tillaegsord/). Supports agreement in gender, number, and definiteness, predicative forms, comparison, and named exceptions.
- **S7 — Adverbs:** [Adverbier, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/adverbier-biord/). Supports adverbial meanings and the fact that most adverbs do not inflect.
- **S8 — Clauses:** [Ledsætninger, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/ledsaetninger/). Supports subordinate-clause status, introducers, fronting, and the diagnostic placement of *ikke*.
- **S9 — Official orthography:** [Retskrivningsordbogen 5.1, Dansk Sprognævn](https://ro.dsn.dk/). This is the official reference for current Danish spelling, inflection, and punctuation; individual entries remain necessary when content records are authored.
- **S10 — Clause and comma grammar:** [Kommagrammatik, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/komma/kommagrammatik/). Supports coordination, subordination, subordinate-clause functions, clause boundaries, and punctuation analysis.
- **S11 — Main and subordinate topology:** [Lindegaard and Sandst, *Om omvendt ledstilling i negerede betingelsesledsætninger*, Aarhus University](https://projekter.au.dk/fileadmin/projekter/Muds.dk/rapporter/MUDS_18.pdf), especially printed pp. 359–361 and 373. Supports the ordinary finite-verb–subject–adverb order in relevant main-clause configurations and subject–adverb–finite-verb order in subordinate clauses. Its marked conditional pattern places negation before the subject; the primary TV-debate analysis is supplemented by written-corpus observations, not a neutral production rule.
- **S12 — Modern subordinate word order:** [Dalberg, *Topologiske forandringer i den danske ledsætning*, University of Copenhagen](https://nors.ku.dk/kalender/2022/gramino-3/abstracts/Dalberg_Topologiske_forandringer_i_den_danske_leds_tning_Fra_VX_til_XV_og_tilbage_igen_Gramino3.pdf). Supports subject–sentence-adverb–finite-verb as a characteristic modern Danish subordinate-clause order and provides historical context.
- **S13 — Comma rules:** [Retskrivningsordbogen sections 45–51, Dansk Sprognævn](https://ro.dsn.dk/?sektion=45-51&type=rulesearch). Supports current rules, including the optional start comma and required clause-boundary decisions.
- **S14 — Conditional meaning:** [Jensen, *Irrealitetsmarkører i dansk talesprog*, University of Copenhagen archive](https://lingvistkredsen.ku.dk/udgivelser/travaux/arkiv/tclc_33/), especially pp. 13 and 81–83. Supports open and hypothetical *hvis* constructions and the relationship between tense displacement and degrees of hypothetical or counterfactual interpretation.
- **S15 — Reflexive possession:** [Hans, hendes eller sin, sproget.dk](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/hans-hendes-eller-sin/). Supports the traditional standard-Danish singular-subject rule, plural *deres*, local clause boundaries, and documented exceptions and variation.

- **S16 — Reported tense perspective:** [Barfod, *Talegengivelse i dansk og tysk*, RUC, 2009](https://rucforsk.ruc.dk/ws/files/57604670/Talegengivelse_i_dansk_og_tysk.pdf#page=35), printed pp. 33–36. This university-hosted master’s thesis supplies supplementary descriptive discussion and corpus observations of context-sensitive tense choices. It is not official normative guidance or specialist curriculum approval.

The concise learner-facing pages from Sproget.dk are used here for the first rule map; production content should additionally verify each stored word form in the current Retskrivningsordbogen and record the exact entry or rule section.
