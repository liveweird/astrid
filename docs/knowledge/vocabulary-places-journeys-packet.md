# P3 Vocabulary Authoring Packet: Places and a Simple Journey

## Status and boundary

This is the bounded P3 continuation of the [selected vocabulary expansion
plan](vocabulary-course-blueprint.md#selected-expansion-authoring-plan--11-november-2026). It proposes
**exactly 21 selected-meaning or chunk placements** and six short session outlines with introduction
batches of **`4 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 3`**. The intended transfer outcome is to read a short
location/journey message, distinguish a destination from a static location, and assemble one
constrained present-tense journey statement.

This packet remains authoring material, not runtime activation. The running private preview is app
0.20.0 on learning pair seventeen with 49 lexical records after the separate P1 release; P3 itself
remains unactivated. S14 remains paused. No stable lexical or sense IDs are allocated here, no current
generator or grader is broadened, and no Danish specialist approval is claimed. Existing E007/E008
recall remains noun-only; count nouns are taught as article+noun bundles, but Q27 and any
article-bundle assessment operation remain open.

The blueprint's **5 reuse + 16 new** split is retained exactly. Reuse means the same selected sense,
not merely the same spelling and not a new construction permission: current `L016-S01`, `L029-S01`,
`L032-S01`, `L038-S01` and P1 row `P1-17` remain their owners. The candidates form one small journey
through transport, stations and two useful destinations; they are not a transport/place taxonomy,
frequency list or claim of comprehensive beginner coverage.

### Placement and grammar audit

- Reuse: **5** (`P3-01`, `P3-04`, `P3-09`–`P3-11`). New candidates: **16**
  (all other rows). Total: **21**.
- Introduction batches are exactly **4/4/4/3/3/3**, covering P3-01–P3-21 once in order.
- Seven new count bundles are `en cykel`, `en station`, `et bibliotek`, `en bus`, `et tog`,
  `en billet`, and `et apotek`. Their articles are taught, not independently assessed.
- Five new action bundles are `gå, køre, cykle, vente, skifte`. The cue and situation must select
  the intended action; a broad English “go” cue cannot make another travel verb wrong.
- Four new direction/location constituents are `til stationen`, static `på stationen`,
  `til apoteket`, and directional `hjem`. Reused `hjemme` is static. Treating these as whole
  constituents avoids claiming productive preposition choice or definite morphology.
- Already available support is limited to human subjects, finite present forms, fixed identification,
  possession, the two selected city names and subject-first clauses. Reuse does not broaden an old
  frame. Direct entry must still teach every P3 placement used in that session before assessment.
- Held-out grammar includes productive definite nouns, arbitrary building prepositions, origin/from
  phrases, transport means/boarding, separable route descriptions, object-taking `skifte/vente`,
  past tense and unrestricted circumstance ordering. Speaker labels, punctuation and the English rail
  situation are supplied support, not hidden placements.

## Six short session outlines

These are future authoring slots, not executable current plans. Every session provides its own required
teaching, including on direct entry. Later sessions may visibly review earlier rows without increasing
the placement count. The readings and prompts are original Astrid drafts awaiting teaching review.
Choice banks stay small where four honest alternatives are unavailable; no unrelated semantic category
is inserted merely to reach four options.

### Session 1 — Two ways to travel (`4`: P3-01–P3-04)

**Real-life outcome:** understand a three-line note that names a car and bicycle and says who is
travelling by bicycle now.

Teaching presents reused `en bil` and `nu`, then new `en cykel` and `at cykle → cykler`. The bicycle
verb is taught through an explicit bicycle-travel scene, not as a generic translation of “go.”

Reading:

> Anna har en bil.  
> Bicycle display: Det er en cykel.  
> Nora cykler nu.

Planned slots:

1. Danish→English recognition: `en cykel` with `a bicycle / a car / a station`; answer
   **a bicycle**. The full count bundle remains visible.
2. Three-pair matching: `en bil ↔ a car`, `en cykel ↔ a bicycle`, `cykler ↔ travels by bicycle`;
   answer is the three exact pairs.
3. Reading choice: “Who is travelling by bicycle now?” with `Nora / Anna`; answer **Nora**.
4. All-word tiles after the worked example: `[Nora] [cykler] [nu]`; answer
   **`Nora cykler nu.`** The period is supplied.

Exposure rule: the teaching cards and reading record the two count bundles, selected cycling sense,
present form and `nu` before the tile slot. Recognition alone is not production evidence. `kører` is
not yet introduced; later, it must not be used as a gratuitous wrong alternative to `cykler` without a
vehicle-specific cue.

### Session 2 — Walking to the station and home (`4`: P3-05–P3-08)

**Real-life outcome:** identify a station destination and distinguish walking to that destination from
walking homeward.

Teaching presents `en station`, `at gå → går`, whole destination `til stationen`, and directional
`hjem`. It shows `stationen` only inside the destination chunk.

Reading:

> Det er en station.  
> Anna går til stationen.  
> Nora går hjem.

Planned slots:

1. Four-pair matching: `en station ↔ a station`, `går ↔ walks/goes on foot`,
   `til stationen ↔ to the station`, `hjem ↔ home/homeward`; answer is the four exact pairs.
2. Reading choice: “Where is Anna going?” with `to the station / home`; answer
   **to the station**.
3. Closed meaning choice: cue `Nora går hjem.` and ask whether `hjem` gives a destination/direction
   or a static location; answer **destination/direction**.
4. All-word tiles: `[Anna] [går] [til stationen]`; answer
   **`Anna går til stationen.`** The chunk remains one pedagogical unit.

Exposure rule: production follows visible exposure to the complete proposition. `til en station`,
`på stationen` and a standalone typed `stationen` are not accepted alternatives in this slot; unknown
text stays unknown rather than being declared bad Danish. The fixed chunk does not teach why the noun
is definite.

### Session 3 — City, home and library (`4`: P3-09–P3-12)

**Real-life outcome:** read a short place note and identify residence, a static home location and a
named public place.

Teaching visibly reviews static `hjemme`, `at bo → bor`, supplied `i Aarhus/i Odense`, and introduces
`et bibliotek`. P3 does not assume completion of P1 or S09. This draft selects complete teaching on every
direct entry; shortened teaching based on prior records would require a separate future decision.

Reading:

> Anna bor i Odense.  
> Nora arbejder hjemme.  
> Det er et bibliotek.

Planned slots:

1. Four-pair matching: `bor ↔ lives/resides`, `i Odense ↔ in Odense`, `hjemme ↔ at home`,
   `et bibliotek ↔ a library`; answer is the four exact pairs.
2. Reading choice: “Where does Anna live?” with `in Odense / at home / at the station`; answer
   **in Odense**. The station option is English only, not an asserted fact or an introduction of Danish `på stationen`.
3. Reading choice: “Where does Nora work?” with `at home / in Odense`; answer **at home**.
4. Static/directional contrast after both are taught: `hjemme` ↔ **static at home** and `hjem` ↔
   **homeward destination**.

Exposure rule: the matching and reading expose the exact static uses. The final contrast records two
distinct selected forms; it is not a free substitution exercise. `på biblioteket`, `til biblioteket`
and `bor hjemme` are outside this packet's permissions and must not be inferred from the theme.

### Session 4 — Ticket, bus and train (`3`: P3-13–P3-15)

**Real-life outcome:** understand a minimal station note that identifies two transport vehicles and
says that a traveller has a ticket.

Teaching presents `en bus`, transport-sense `et tog`, and `en billet`. The source/teaching card calls
out that P3's `tog` is a train, not the procession homograph.

Reading:

> Anna har en billet.  
> Vehicle A: Det er en bus.  
> Vehicle B: Det er et tog.

Planned slots:

1. Three-pair matching: `en bus ↔ a bus`, `et tog ↔ a train`, `en billet ↔ a ticket`; answer is
   the three exact pairs.
2. Reading choice: “What does Anna have?” with `a ticket / a bus / a train`; answer **a ticket**.
3. English→Danish closed choice: “Choose the introduced bundle for **a train**” with
   `en bus / et tog / en billet`; answer **`et tog`**.

Exposure rule: all three article+noun surfaces are recognition/matching evidence, not typed bundle
recall. The reading does not say that Anna owns either vehicle or that her ticket is valid for one of
them. No `med bus/tog`, definite vehicle or boarding language is silently introduced.

### Session 5 — Waiting at the station (`3`: P3-16–P3-18)

**Real-life outcome:** read where one person waits and understand that another traveller is moving
homeward by vehicle.

Teaching presents vehicular `at køre → kører`, `at vente → venter`, and whole static chunk
`på stationen`. It explicitly contrasts static `på stationen` with destination `til stationen`, while
leaving productive preposition choice for later grammar. The worked situation states in English that
Nora is travelling home by car; the bare Danish sentence is not used to teach a named vehicle phrase.

Reading:

> Anna venter på stationen.  
> Nora kører hjem.

Planned slots:

1. Three-pair matching: `kører ↔ travels/drives by vehicle`, `venter ↔ waits`,
   `på stationen ↔ at the station`; answer is the three exact pairs.
2. Reading choice: “Where does Anna wait?” with `at the station / to the station`; answer
   **at the station**.
3. Destination/static contrast: `til stationen` → **destination**, `på stationen` →
   **static location**.
4. Present-form recall with explicit scene cue “The person is travelling home by car. Type the
   introduced present form of `at køre`”; answer **`kører`**. `cykler` is a known different action,
   not bad Danish.

Exposure rule: the reading exposes complete `venter på stationen` and `kører hjem` propositions
before recall. A bare English “goes home” cue is forbidden because it would not fairly select `kører`
over another motion verb. `venter til stationen`, an object after `venter`, or an unseen vehicle phrase
remains outside the packet rather than receiving a broad grammar diagnosis.

### Session 6 — A change and the pharmacy (`3`: P3-19–P3-21)

**Real-life outcome:** read an English-supported rail message, identify a connection change, and
assemble one constrained statement about walking to a pharmacy.

Teaching presents transport-change `at skifte → skifter`, `et apotek`, and whole destination
`til apoteket`. The first sentence is preceded by the supplied English situation “Anna is travelling
by train”; that context is required to select the transport-change sense without adding new Danish
vocabulary.

Reading:

> Anna skifter i Aarhus.  
> Nora går til apoteket.  
> Anna går hjem.

Planned slots:

1. Three-pair matching: `skifter ↔ changes trains/connections`, `et apotek ↔ a pharmacy`,
   `til apoteket ↔ to the pharmacy`; answer is the three exact pairs.
2. Context reading choice: “What does Anna do in Aarhus?” with `changes trains / waits at the
   station / walks home`; answer **changes trains**. The rail situation is part of the prompt.
3. Reading choice: “Where does Nora walk?” with `to the pharmacy / home`; answer
   **to the pharmacy**.
4. Final constrained transfer tiles: `[Jeg] [går] [til apoteket]`; answer
   **`Jeg går til apoteket.`** The first-person proposition is new, but every selected sense, form and
   chunk has already been exposed.

Exposure rule: the rail-context card, not `skifter` alone, establishes the selected connection sense.
Final production requires saved exposure to the exact destination chunk; knowing `gå`, `til` or
`et apotek` independently is insufficient. `Jeg går på apoteket`, free route descriptions, medicine
requests and object-taking `skifter` remain unknown/out of scope, not automatically wrong Danish.

## Shared answer, exposure and review rules

- Every scored slot has one primary target. Supplied subjects, identification/possession frames,
  English situation text and punctuation are support and do not become learned placements.
- A displayed introduced alternative that misses the requested meaning is known and off-target, not
  invalid Danish. An unintroduced form, synonym or construction is unknown unless a narrow response
  constraint alone decides the result. Raw typed input must be retained in a future implementation.
- Count bundles remain intact in teaching and matching. No session adds a typed article+noun target or
  changes E008. Definite forms inside chunks are possible exposure to those visible forms, but not
  evidence that productive definiteness has been taught.
- Opening a session is not completion or learning evidence. A future plan must record visible selected
  senses, forms, complete propositions, answer reveals and feedback. Direct entry always supplies full required
  teaching in this draft; no cross-run exposure shortcut is selected.
- Matching cardinality is three or four according to the honest bank. The selected P1 implementation
  adds bounded mixed-meaning boards; P3 still needs its own new authored pools and frozen plans.
  Neither P1 nor this draft broadens retained E014 or other published pools.
- Before a proposed private-preview activation, complete bounded source/content review of meanings,
  article bundles, location/direction contrasts, readings, accepted alternatives and feedback. Keep
  the naturalness of `kører hjem` and rail-context `skifter i Aarhus` visible for specialist review
  (BL-022); pending specialist approval does not impose a new human gate on draft preview work.
  Runtime design must allocate stable identities, preserve old revisions/fingerprints and prove
  direct-entry/exposure behavior. P3 activation remains unselected.

## Source method and review limits

On 11 November 2026 the sixteen prefetched candidate lemma responses were read from the official COR
lookup service and the selected active record IDs were checked row by row above. A narrow official
lookup additionally confirmed preposition `på` as `COR.00190.880.01`; it was not part of the prefetched
set. The transport record for `tog` is deliberately `COR.51979`, not procession `COR.48221`; verb
records are used for `køre`, `vente` and `skifte`, not their noun/fixed-phrase homographs. No dataset was
downloaded or imported. [COR release](https://ordregister.dk/) ·
[lookup documentation](https://ordregister.dk/doc/COR.Webservices.html)

DDO's human-walking use of `gå`, vehicular-motion use of `køre`, bicycle-travel use of `cykle`, waiting
use of `vente`, and transport-change use of `skifte` were consulted as bounded sense/frame references.
They support the distinctions above but do not certify every authored sentence or the teaching order.
In particular, `køre` is broader than `cykle`, so assessment must use a vehicle-specific cue rather than
calling `cykler` wrong; intransitive `skifter i Aarhus` requires the explicit rail context recorded in
session 6. No dictionary definition or corpus sentence is copied into this packet.

The six readings contain **3/3/3/3/2/3 original sentences**. This packet therefore completes the P3
authoring allocation and is ready for bounded source/content review, not runtime activation, app-version
change, migration, profile continuation, S14 resumption or a claim of learner effectiveness.
