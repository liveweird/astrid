# Research and Sources

These references were consulted during the initial planning discussion. They support specific language facts or possible design directions; they do not establish an adopted curriculum or a complete exercise-generation system.

## Danish grammar references

- [Sproget.dk on sentences](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/saetninger/): introductory sentence components and main versus subordinate clauses.
- [Sproget.dk on clause analysis](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/komma/kommagrammatik/): identifying subordinate clauses through grammatical analysis, rather than relying only on introductory words.
- [Sproget.dk on adjectives](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/adjektiver-tillaegsord/): gender, number, definiteness, comparison, and selected exceptions.

Use these as reference starting points. The proposed structural inventory requires broader coverage and linguistic review.

The [grammar map](danish-grammar-map.md#source-register) now records 16 grammatical reference sources, including Danish Language Council material and university research on word order and conditionals. Supplementary thesis evidence is labeled separately from normative guidance. The [construction catalogue](danish-sentence-catalogue.md#source-basis) distinguishes neutral beginner models from later spoken/register variation. Their teaching order and original examples remain drafts requiring specialist review; the [foundation source review](foundation-source-review.md) records corrections and remaining gaps.

## Vocabulary and morphology data

[Det Centrale Ordregister documentation](https://ordregister.dk/doc/COR.html) describes Danish lemma and word-form identifiers, morphological information, and accepted form variants. The base resource is freely available for reuse. Other COR resources can have different terms, so record the exact resource, version, provenance, and licence when selecting data.

The [COR resource catalogue](https://ordregister.dk/) provides downloads and information about extensions. Treat COR as a candidate source for linguistic data. The app still needs curated teaching vocabulary, English meanings, usage constraints, and links to curriculum topics.

No dataset has been imported or selected as the definitive vocabulary source. A downloadable resource and a pedagogically sufficient vocabulary subset are separate deliverables.

For the first [23-entry vocabulary seed](danish-vocabulary-seed.md), Den Danske Ordbog entries were consulted for selected noun genders, inflected forms, verb forms, and meanings/frames. The seed links each noun/verb to its lookup and distinguishes homonyms and senses. English glosses, semantic allowlists, and examples are original teaching proposals; no dictionary definitions, corpus quotations, images, or audio were imported. A corpus-based frequency selection and dataset-licensing decision are still outstanding.

## Learning and outcomes

- [Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-companion-volume-and-its-language-versions): reading and written-production descriptors can help define outcomes. CEFR alignment and a target level have not been decided.
- [Carpenter, Pan, and Butler, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00089-1): research review of spacing and retrieval practice. It supports considering these strategies, not a particular scheduling algorithm or proof that this app's design will work.

## Research still needed

- Extend and validate the first broad grammar/construction inventory, including its documented coverage gaps.
- A reviewed prerequisite structure and teaching sequence for English speakers.
- Appropriate frequency and usage evidence for selecting vocabulary.
- Sources for English equivalents, verb constructions, and semantic compatibility.
- Resource-specific reuse terms for any definitions, examples, or other content incorporated into the product.
- Teacher review of explanations and examples, followed by learner trials of the proposed exercises.

## Resource audit update — 7 November 2026

The [Danish resource assessment](danish-resource-assessment.md) records current official COR/RO, COR.EXT, COR.SEM and DDO evidence, versions and published reuse terms. It proposes a COR-first morphology audit while preserving authored English senses and internal IDs. No resource has been imported or adopted as a runtime dependency; current-release terms and exact mappings remain open.

Independent area research and lead validation extended this to [frequency/usage candidates](frequency-and-usage-resources.md) and [English–Danish lexical candidates](bilingual-resources.md). They distinguish corpus editions, morphological versus semantic assertions, data versus tool licences and conditional reuse. No publisher contact or product import was performed. The [pilot variation audit](pilot-variation-audit.md) measures the authored fixture separately from external frequency evidence.
