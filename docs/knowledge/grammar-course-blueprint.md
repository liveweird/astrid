# Seven-Module Danish Grammar Course Blueprint

## Status and scope

**8 November 2026 — User-supplied curriculum proposal.** The user described a preferred seven-module sequence with 28 lessons, labelled A1.1 through B2, and four architectural principles. This document preserves that proposal and separately records assistant recommendations and bounded source checks. Numbered versions of Modules 3, 5 and 7 supersede their earlier unnumbered messages.

The labels express the proposed progression, not verified CEFR alignment, an official Danish course, or a promise of B2 proficiency after 28 sessions. **User-confirmed clarification:** the 28 lessons are topic units, each containing multiple short practice sessions (Q26). Reading and writing remain the active product scope; later conversational pragmatics can be studied through written dialogues. Listening and speaking remain postponed.

The [58-skill map](danish-grammar-map.md) and [30-construction catalogue](danish-sentence-catalogue.md) remain the detailed authoring inventory. Course numbers below do not replace stable G/C/L identifiers or alter their prerequisite graph. Only the existing beginner subset is implemented; this proposal does not authorize new runtime content or exercise development. Appropriate Danish teaching review remains outstanding (BL-022).

## User's architectural principles

1. **Present verbs and V2 together in Module 1:** enable immediate sentence building while introducing fronting and inversion early; avoid a course that presents subject-first order as the whole Danish system.
2. **Nouns before adjectives:** teach gender, number and definiteness before the corresponding adjective-agreement patterns. The user proposes mastering suffixed definiteness first; the prerequisite distinction for basic versus definite adjectives is discussed below.
3. **Possessive reference deserves explicit treatment:** distinguish ordinary possessives from `sin/sit/sine` in a dedicated syntactic context after object-pronoun work, rather than burying the contrast in a vocabulary list.
4. **Introduce a simplified sentence schema early:** use Diderichsen-style front, middle and content fields to build a consistent explanation of finite verbs, sentence adverbs and later subordinate clauses. The claim that this makes later syntax effortless is an aspiration, not an observed learning result.

## Source checks and qualifications

These are bounded checks, not a complete validation of every example or the teaching sequence.

- **Morphological simplicity:** present verbs do not need separate person/number paradigms in this standard beginner model, but “just add `-r`” is a regular pattern, not a complete lexicon. Keep irregular/modal forms explicit. Do not describe nouns as wholly case-free without acknowledging genitive formation; the Council's guide calls it genitive/ejefald. [DSN verbs](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/verber-udsagnord/), [DSN genitive](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/genitiv-ejefald/).
- **Gender and plural forms:** the approximate 75% common-gender figure is supported by a DSN answer, which also warns that gender tendencies are not fixed rules. Teach each selected noun with its article. The plural inventory should explicitly include `-r` as in `æble → æbler`, alongside `-er`, `-e`, zero and stored irregular forms. “Universal `-s`” must allow the documented apostrophe rules for words ending in `s/x/z` and other special cases. [DSN gender](https://sproget.dk/sprogviden/spoergsmaal-og-svar/dansk-sprognaevn/sv00000096/), [DSN nouns](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/substantiver-navneord/), [DSN genitive](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/genitiv-ejefald/).
- **Adjectives:** keep attributive definiteness separate from predicative agreement: a definite subject does not force adjective `-e` (`Huset er stort`). Gender supports basic indefinite/predicative agreement; mastering suffixed definiteness is not logically required for `et stort hus`. That prerequisite is specifically useful for contrasting definite phrase patterns. This prerequisite interpretation is an assistant recommendation based on the examples, not a source-prescribed teaching order. [DSN adjectives](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/adjektiver-tillaegsord/).
- **Verb fields:** V2 counts constituents, not words, and applies here to neutral main declaratives; yes/no questions use finite-verb-first order. A non-finite verb occupies a later field, not necessarily the end of the sentence: the user's `Jeg har ikke set filmen endnu` already has material after `set`. Use the [catalogue's field notation](danish-sentence-catalogue.md#how-to-read-the-patterns) rather than an absolute “participle last” rule. [University of Copenhagen word-order account](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/ordstilling-i-leds%C3%A6tninger-i-moderne-dansk-talesprog/).
- **Perfect auxiliaries:** a motion verb does not always require `er`: compare arrival/departure readings with an ongoing movement activity using `har`. Auxiliary choice depends on the selected sense/frame and event meaning, including telicity and transitivity; store licensed combinations rather than infer the answer from a motion tag. [DSN on auxiliary choice](https://dsn.dk/nyt-fra-sprognaevnet/maj-2024/have-eller-vaere-abdiceret/).
- **Possessive reference:** initially contrast local singular third-person subjects with other possessors; plural third-person subjects ordinarily use `deres`. Hidden clauses, regional variation and perspective need later explicit scope. Do not apply a universal same-clause substitution algorithm. [DSN on hans/hendes/sin](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/hans-hendes-eller-sin/).
- **Subordination:** `S–adverb–finite verb` is the initial written production model. Avoid claiming that every subordinate clause universally prohibits V2: Danish research documents embedded main-clause order, particularly in speech, with information-structure effects. This does not license freely interchangeable beginner answers. [DSN subordinate clauses](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/ledsaetninger/), [Christensen et al., 2020](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/foregrounding-of-subordinate-clauses-by-word-order-psycholinguist/).
- **Passive agreement:** the user's `Bilerne blev solgte/solgt` must not become a free-alternative grading rule for an event passive. DSN distinguishes uninflected verbal/process participles from adjectival state agreement, with permitted variation in some contexts. Start event examples with `Bilerne blev solgt i går` and author adjectival/state contrasts separately. [DSN participle guide](https://sproget.dk/typiske-problemer/t-eller-de/).
- **Passive choice and lexical combinations:** general versus individual-event readings are useful tendencies for `-s` versus `blive`, not an exclusive rule; DDO explicitly describes their distribution non-categorically. Treat particle/compound verbs as separate lexical constructions, not a productive split/join transformation. The proposed modern `opgå` example was not verified and needs replacement or specialist confirmation before teaching use. [DDO blive, sense 1](https://ordnet.dk/ddo/ordbog/11005152), [DDO omtale, verb entry](https://ordnet.dk/ddo/ordbog/omtale).
- **CEFR and learning claims:** preserve the user's A1–B2 labels as tentative course bands. The CEFR describes communicative competencies; language-specific grammar specifications require a separate mapping. Neither 28 headings nor a sentence-field explanation demonstrates proficiency, prevents fossilization by itself, or guarantees effortless later learning. [Council of Europe reference-level descriptions](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/reference-level-descriptions), [CEFR grammatical competence, printed p. 113](https://rm.coe.int/1680459f97).

## Recommended reconciliation — assistant proposal

Keep the seven-module outline as the course-navigation draft and split broad lessons into short steps with one assessed target, consistent with the user's confirmed topic-unit clarification. In particular, lessons 4, 8, 11, 16, 20 and 26 bundle distinct operations or grammatical systems. Preserve these 28 parent topics; no number or duration of sessions, pass threshold or scheduler is selected.

Use a spiral introduction: Module 1 can supply fixed noun phrases with `en/et`, time/place chunks and basic modal expressions before later modules explain their full paradigms. Introduce useful `der er`, simple prepositions and coordination in bounded early contexts, then revisit advanced analysis later. These are teaching recommendations rather than an alteration of the user's module order or current runtime scope.

Reconcile the prerequisite graph explicitly: G021 currently depends on G016/G018; early referential `den/det` cannot inherit every prerequisite of the combined G038 target; modal sentences need bare-infinitive support. Keep adjective reference and noun support supplied when not assessed. Subject wh questions need separate handling from the simple wh–verb–subject template.

The detailed inventory also contains useful topics not explicit in the 28 headings: imperatives (G020), indirect questions (G048), indefinite/negative pronouns (G040), numerals/quantifiers (G041), infinitive `at` complements (G031), and writing conventions (G001/G056/G057). Decide which are embedded in lessons and which need additional subtopics rather than claiming the outline is exhaustive.

Prioritize explicit new coverage for direction/location, posture frames, clefts, modal particles, polarity responses and the cited verb-particle/transitivity pairs. Do not assign new G/C IDs until each target and its scope are defined. The [coverage crosswalk](foundation-coverage.md) remains authoritative for existing draft gaps.

For each resulting step, author the target, prerequisites, licensed structures and senses, learner explanation, accepted alternatives, contextual constraints, common errors, compatible exercise families, and an unseen reading/writing outcome. Optional lesson/hint support must not accidentally reveal an assessed answer. Preserve meaning, form, task compliance and uncertainty as separate grading facets. See the [exercise inventory](exercise-family-expansion.md#user-exercise-inventory--8-november-2026) and [outcome record](beginner-outcome-reference.md#proposed-course-authoring-record).
