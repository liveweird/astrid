## How to read the exposure audit

Every session is independently enterable. It visibly teaches its own batch and visibly reviews every
Danish alternative or prerequisite used later in that session. It never assumes completion, exposure or
learning from P1, another P2 session, browser history or a global inventory.

The backticked exposure labels below are **descriptive audit labels only**, not proposed stable runtime
keys. Their prefixes have these exact meanings:

- `surface:` — the exact Danish text is visible;
- `meaning:` — that visible Danish surface is explicitly mapped to the stated English selected meaning;
- `bundle:` — the whole count/mass/quantity teaching shape is visible as a unit;
- `form:` — an infinitive/present relation is visibly explained;
- `frame:` — the complete bounded order or construction is shown and translated;
- `fact:` — the complete translated proposition or scene relation is visible;
- `support:` — visible material needed to enter the task but not owned as a P2 target.

An English-only distractor exposes no Danish counterpart. A Danish choice or tile exposes its exact
surface, but a shuffled matching prompt does not expose the correct bilingual correspondence until the
learner has made a correct pair or a solution is actually rendered. A reading exposes its rendered finite
form and proposition, not an unseen infinitive. Seeing a solution or retry may be recorded as exposure,
never as proof of learning or independent recall.

Every bilingual matching board uses the established **English-left / Danish-right** `meaning/en-da`
direction. Danish→English recognition choices remain in their separately stated direction.

For all closed-choice slots, an unknown/duplicate option identifier or response outside the displayed
bank is malformed input after ownership checks, not a learner-language judgment. For tiles, only the
listed answer is accepted. A listed reviewed alternative can be known and off-target; any other complete
order or added text remains linguistically **unknown**, even when response shape can independently be
declared unmet. “Unknown” never means “bad Danish.”

## Session 1 — A small breakfast

**Placement ownership:** P2-01 `spise` (reuse), P2-02 `et æble` (reuse), P2-03 bare mass `brød`
(new), P2-04 isolated `billig` (new). **Outcome:** understand the stated breakfast objects and recognize
one low-price product label. There is no adjective-agreement or adjective-placement lesson.

### Direct-entry teaching script

1. Show `at spise → spiser` and say: “`at spise` means **to eat**. `spiser` means **eats/is
   eating** and is the present form used in today's reading.”
   Exposure: `surface:at spise`, `surface:spiser`, `meaning:spise=to eat`,
   `meaning:spiser=eats/is eating`, `form:spise→spiser`.
2. Show `et æble` and say: “`et æble` means **an apple**. This count noun is taught with `et`; keep
   the bundle together.”
   Exposure: `surface:et æble`, `meaning:et æble=an apple`, `bundle:et æble=count`.
3. Show `brød` and say: “Bare `brød` means **bread** in this selected food use. This session does
   not put `en` or `et` before it.”
   Exposure: `surface:brød`, `meaning:brød=bread`, `bundle:brød=bare mass`.
4. Show an English-labelled low-price product card, then show `billig`. Say: “On this card, `billig`
   means **inexpensive**. We use only this supplied base-form label. The English alternatives
   **expensive** and **fresh** do not teach Danish words.”
   Exposure: `surface:billig`, `meaning:billig=inexpensive`.
5. Review `jeg = I` as supplied support and explain that Nora is a person's name. Show the supported
   order “person + present verb + food.”
   Exposure: `surface:jeg`, `support:jeg=I`, `support:Nora=name`,
   `support:subject-present-food order`.
6. Show and read the exact text:

   > Jeg spiser et æble.  
   > Nora spiser brød.

   Give the exact translation: **“I eat an apple. Nora eats bread.”**
   Exposure: `surface:Jeg spiser et æble.`, `surface:Nora spiser brød.`,
   `frame:Jeg|spiser|et æble`, `frame:Nora|spiser|brød`,
   `fact:speaker|eats|an apple`, `fact:Nora|eats|bread`.

## Session 3 — Getting attention and receiving a roll

**Placement ownership:** P2-09 `undskyld`, P2-10 `værsgo`, P2-11 `en bolle`, P2-12 `en butik`
(all new). **Outcome:** recognize the shop and roll and distinguish the two functional expressions by

### Direct-entry teaching script

1. Show an English-labelled scene: **“A customer politely wants the shopkeeper's attention.”** Then
   show `Undskyld.` and explain: “Here `undskyld` means **excuse me**, used to get attention. This is

   Exposure: `surface:Undskyld.`, `meaning:undskyld=excuse me (getting attention)`,
   `fact:customer|seeks shopkeeper attention|Undskyld`.
2. Show a different English-labelled scene: **“The shopkeeper hands the stated roll to the customer.”**
   Then show `Værsgo.` and explain: “Here `værsgo` means **here you are**, while handing the item
   over.”
   Exposure: `surface:Værsgo.`, `meaning:værsgo=here you are (handover)`,
   `fact:shopkeeper|hands over roll|Værsgo`.
3. Show `en bolle = a bread roll`; keep `en` with the count noun. Review bare `brød = bread` so the
   later object alternative is visible and its mass/count boundary is explicit.
   Exposure: `surface:en bolle`, `meaning:en bolle=a bread roll`, `bundle:en bolle=count`,
   `surface:brød`, `meaning:brød=bread`, `bundle:brød=bare mass`.
4. Show `en butik = a shop`; keep `en` with the count noun. Show supplied `Det er … = This is …`
   support only for the exact identification below.
   Exposure: `surface:en butik`, `meaning:en butik=a shop`, `bundle:en butik=count`,

5. Review `spiser = eats/is eating` and explain that Nora is a name.

## Session 4 — Buying and paying

**Placement ownership:** P2-13 `en café`, P2-14 `købe → køber`, P2-15 `betale → betaler` (all

   Exposure: `surface:at købe`, `surface:køber`, `meaning:købe=to buy`,
   `meaning:køber=buys/is buying`, `form:købe→køber`.
3. Show `at betale → betaler`: “`at betale` means **to pay**. `betaler` means **pays/is
   paying**. In this exact situation the item is already established, so `Jeg betaler.` is complete.”
   Exposure: `surface:at betale`, `surface:betaler`, `meaning:betale=to pay`,
   `meaning:betaler=pays/is paying`, `form:betale→betaler`,

4. Review `jeg = I`, `en bolle = a bread roll`, `spiser = eats/is eating`, and `drikker =
